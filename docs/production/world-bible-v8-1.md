## PART ZERO — HARD RULES

These rules override every other consideration. Violating any is out of scope.

Rule 1 — No "power of friendship" endings. Every outcome is complicity.

Rule 2 — Heaviness throughout, not saved for endings. The whole game carries weight.

Rule 3 — The sacrifice-ritual ending has no escape hatch. When the player commits to the sacrifice of Elara, the game commits with them. No reload-out. No secret third option at the moment of commitment. The player chose this.

Rule 4 — The Witness is small, quiet, documentary at surface. Older and darker beneath. The apparent register is a functionary with a blade; the true register, as it unfolds, is something far older. The transition from one to the other is the game. Combat powers exist and scale (Rule 4 governs character register, not combat-system design).

Rule 5 — The ship is a pen, not a sword. Impartial Regard has no offensive weapons. Chaff-launcher only. Ship combat is eliminated from scope.

Rule 6 — One Witness, always. Only one. There has only ever been one Witness. Not one at a time across many generations — one single being across all of recorded and unrecorded history. Summoned when needed, sleeps otherwise. The player is his current summoning.

Rule 7 — Cosmic weight, not cosmic clock. The Black Star's presence is atmospheric and structural, not mechanical. There is no ticking doomsday. The game is not about stopping the Black Star. It is about what the Witness does once he understands he has been serving it, and always has been.

Rule 8 — Low-fantasy commitment. Most of the galaxy is mundane. The Gift is rare and concentrated. The cosmic/religious weight is felt in Order spaces and in deep-lore investigation, not distributed evenly across the world. The cult is the horror. The general population does not experience the cult.

Rule 9 — Two-layer game structure. The surface game (nine-claimant succession crisis) is complete without the deep game (Witness's identity, the Black Star, the ritual). Players who pull the deep-lore thread discover a second game beneath the first. The surface game never requires understanding the deep game, but the deep game recontextualizes everything.

Rule 10 — The Long Record cannot be unwritten. Whatever is committed to it is committed. Save = write. Death without recent writing = the next waking inherits consequences without context.

Rule 11 — The sacrifice of Elara is presented as the galaxy's best outcome. If the player refuses it, they refuse for personal reasons, not obvious moral ones. The Fourth faction's math is real. Some players should choose the sacrifice. Those players must not be wrong. They must only have to live with it.

Rule 12 (NEW in v8.1) — The ritual ending is genuinely hidden. Not gated behind a second playthrough — reachable in one playthrough by a player who pulls hard enough on the deep-lore threads. But the content is distributed such that many players, across many playthroughs, will never find it. The six surface endings are the full game for non-hunters. The chamber figure revelation is optional content. The game does not nudge players toward the deep path after a surface ending; there is no "true ending" rhetoric in the game's presentation of itself.

Rule 13 (NEW in v8.1) — GRRM-route on kills. The player's kills do not feel morally proportional to their choices. Sparing a claimant has its own costs. The moral arithmetic never balances. Every ending is paid for in bodies. Sparing someone the player respects to serve the ending they want is as weighty as killing them.

Rule 14 (NEW in v8.1) — Black Star precedes the Witness. The god has been running the cycle of rebellion-and-power across eternities, through many vessels before the first Witness existed. The Order is a specific, late, institutional response to the first Witness — not the origin of the god's work.

## PART ONE — THE WITNESS

### The Truth of the Witness

There has only ever been one Witness. One figure. One consciousness. One being across all of time *as the Witness.* He was not, however, the Black Star's first instrument. The Black Star worked through other vessels and arrangements across eternities before the first Witness existed. What makes this man the Witness is that the Order formed around him. The role is institutional, not cosmic — it is what the Black Star's work looks like when a specific priesthood has grown up around a specific body.

He exists in two states.

Dormant state — the chamber figure. The sleeping core. Sealed in a chamber beneath the Tessarene motherhouse. Bound in place. The priest-host of the Black Star in its most intimate, permanent form. He does not leave. He does not age meaningfully. He has been there as long as Tessarene has existed, and in other chambers before that.

Operational state — the walking Witness. The waking hand. Dispatched across history when the Black Star calls or when the Order performs the rite. Wakes without memory. Wears the signet. Walks. Does the work. Dies or returns to the chamber when the work is done, and sleeps again.

These are the same being in two modes. The chamber figure is asleep; the walking Witness is awake. When the current waking ends, the consciousness returns to the sleeping core.

### Why He Is in the Chamber

The chamber figure is not in the chamber only to serve as a vessel for summoning. He is also contained there. During his original walking — before there was an Order, before there was a priesthood — he committed specific terrible deeds as the Black Star's instrument. Some of those deeds were the founding acts of iteration one (the establishment of what became the first recorded dynasty). Some were committed during iteration two (the transition to the current Vesh dynasty, including the killing of Miriath). Across both iterations, during the periods when the Black Star was channeling through him most directly — his god-in-flesh periods — he did things that the coalition around him came to regard as too dangerous to allow to continue.

The Order, in its earliest institutional form, grew up partly as priesthood and partly as containment apparatus. The men and women who would become the first Order decided, after observing what he became during god-in-flesh periods, that he could not be left loose and could not be killed. He was too useful to the cycle, too load-bearing, too integral to the Black Star's work. So they sealed him. They made the chamber. They developed the rite of summoning. The Order's founding purpose is dual — serve the god's cycle, and keep the god's most intimate vessel bound.

The chamber figure consented to this, originally. The circumstances of that consent — what he agreed to, for what reasons — are lost even to himself. The sleep has eroded his own memory of himself. What remains in the chamber is a man who agreed to become an instrument so long ago that the reasons no longer survive even in his own mind. That is his wound. Not grief. Not guilt in the simple sense. Consent that outlived its context.

### The Original Deeds

During his original walking, the first Witness committed eight specific historical atrocities. These events are on the public record of galactic history. Every educated person in the galaxy has heard of them — the Long List, the Burning of Ashdune, the Ratification at the Nine Rivers, and so on (specific names workshop-pending). They are referenced in textbooks, in liturgy, in the oaths of state institutions, in the folk memory of affected planets. They are neutral historical facts at the surface, the way major atrocities in our own history are neutral historical facts.

What is not on the public record is that these eight events were all the work of a single figure. They are attributed to "the Witness" in the generalized, institutional sense — the same way "the Witness" is understood to have judged succession disputes for centuries, the same way "the Witness" is understood to be a role rather than a man. The public does not know there has been one Witness. The Order does not, outside tier 4. The events and the chamber figure are not connected in the galactic consciousness.

The player hears of these events across the game, neutrally, as history. NPCs reference them. Claimants' realms are shaped by them (see Part Six — the realms' ritual resonances). The player learns them the way the player learns any setting detail.

Only the hunter connects the events to the current Witness. Through the relics (Part Five), through the memory-returns (Part Five), through the Long Record's deeper pages (Part Eight), through Ioran Kest (Part Seven), the hunter assembles the realization that the Witness of the public record is one man and that man is themselves. The non-hunter finishes the game with the events intact as history and no personal connection made.

The eight deeds occur in two registers:

Founding-act deeds — political, documentary, social atrocities. The first Witness as institution-builder. The Long List and similar — moments when he used authority, naming, documentary cruelty to break populations, to bind factions, to establish the founding structures of the cycle he was creating. The relics these deeds produce yield authority-register powers for the current Witness (naming, marking, social unbecoming).

Channeling-act deeds — direct, physical, cosmic-scale atrocities. The first Witness as god-in-flesh. Moments when the Black Star was burning through him most directly and he did things no ordinary person could do. The relics these deeds produce yield combat-register powers for the current Witness (fire, devastating force, the kinds of abilities that make the combat system scale).

The split across the eight is roughly four-and-four. Specific assignments per realm in Part Six, with full workshop pending.

Miriath's killing is a ninth act — iteration two's ritual climax. It is structurally parallel to what Elara's killing would be in iteration three. See Part Five and Part Nine.

### How This Has Been Hidden

The general galaxy believes there are many Witnesses. Each era has its Witness, who dies and is replaced. This is the Order's deliberate deception. Bodies substituted, identities manufactured, funerals held, replacements "arriving" from the novitiate. Only tier 4 initiates know that every Witness who has walked has been the same man.

This is the single largest institutional lie in the galaxy. It is load-bearing for the Order's power.

### The Current Waking

The current waking — the player — is the most recent. He woke some short time before game start, memory blank, body prepared, signet waiting.

He remembers nothing. Not prior wakings. Not who he was before the first walking. Not the deeds, public history or otherwise. Not the names he has used. Not the faces he has worn.

He retains motor memory and language facility. Personality does not carry. Ethics do not. Identity does not. Each waking is a new man in an old body, inheriting only the instincts the body holds.

### The Summoning That Woke Him

The Fourth faction engineered this waking. They killed the prior waking (who had taken the name Adhvi Ren) in a specific manner that forced the body into dormancy, then performed the summoning rite under their own conditions to produce the current waking at a moment of their choosing.

Sevasti, of the Prescient faction, learned shortly after the waking that the Fourth had engineered it. Her counter-move was to redirect the Witness immediately toward Elara — sending the first letter to his desk in Act IV with instructions to retrieve the girl from the Verge. The Witness's entire game exists because the Fourth woke him and Sevasti pointed him at Elara first.

### The Signet

The signet he wears is his own. He has worn it at every waking. It has never left him. The signet remembers what he does not. Dreams visit him from it. Fragments of prior wakings surface when he holds it against certain objects, certain places, certain blood.

The signet's outward form is three concentric rings — a stylized Black Star, the void-at-center and its ripples.

### The Body

Working hypothesis: the Witness has one original body, aging extraordinarily slowly. Across sleeps, the body is suspended. Across wakings, it ages at normal rate for the duration of the waking. Total accumulated waking-time amounts to perhaps two or three human lifetimes by the present. His body shows this — he appears middle-aged to the player, but the small physical details match descriptions in the Long Record from wakings centuries ago.

Elara, in the blood-taking ritual, catches glimpses of him from every prior waking and learns to recognize the body's signature across time.

### The Revelation Drip-Feed

The player does not learn what they are in one scene. The revelation is drip-fed across the game through multiple channels, available only to the hunter who pulls the threads:

Channel 1 — NPC recognition. Specific NPCs remember him, placed on specific planets in specific rooms a casual player will not visit. An elderly woman in a side-room teahouse on a Verge world who served him tea forty years ago. A Damar Hul veteran tucked into a garrison storeroom who fought beside him in the Crown Wars. A child's grandmother in an Orphan World alley who says "he looks the same."

Channel 2 — Environmental storytelling. Placed deliberately off main paths. A journal in a ruined house in his handwriting. A portrait of a man who resembles him in a dead noble's private parlor. A signet in a museum case, the same signet he wears, labeled as belonging to a Witness from eleven hundred years ago.

Channel 3 — The Long Record's deeper pages. Unlocked through Elara's transformation of the eight relics. Each transformation, if accompanied by an environmental memory-trigger in the realm, surfaces a Long Record entry in the Witness's own hand describing one of the eight original deeds.

Channel 4 — Ioran Kest. A prior waking who did not return to the chamber. Alive on a Verge moon. Has been researching his own nature for forty years. Reachable only through environmental breadcrumbs the attentive player can find (a reference in the Long Record, a mention by an old NPC, a name on a shipping manifest in an unrelated location). If reached, he is the tipping-point revealer.

Channel 5 — The chamber meeting itself. The final reveal, in the deep-lore path. The Witness descends beneath Tessarene, past Korien's vault, into the chamber. The figure opens his eyes. The Witness sees his own face.

The drip-feed's pacing: distributed across the whole game with no mechanical gate forcing the player to encounter any specific channel. A player who encounters three of five channels assembles a partial picture. A player who encounters all five, or four including Ioran, reaches full revelation. Most players encounter zero or one.

### The Six Backgrounds

Character creation offers six backgrounds: Former Soldier, Disgraced Theologian, Physician of the Verge, Lapsed Aristocrat, Born in the Orphan Worlds, No Record.

These are cover stories the Order prepared for this waking. None describes the Witness's actual past. "No Record" is closest to truth — the player who picks it will have an easier time accepting what they eventually learn. The others are falsifications. Mid-game, the player who picks a rich fictional background will find contradictions in it. The cover story cracks.

Specific cracking moments per background — how each cover story breaks — is a workshop item (Open Question, Part Fourteen). Each background needs one authored moment where the fabrication visibly fails.

### The Three States Mechanically

Ending state categories differentiate based on what happens to the Witness's two states at the climax:

Standard endings (α, β, γ, δ, ε): The walking Witness finishes his work and returns to the chamber. He sleeps.

ζ — The Sacrifice (hidden path): The walking Witness kills Elara, merges with something older through the ritual, ceases to be just the walking hand. See Part Nine.

η — Kill the chamber figure: The walking Witness kills the sleeping core. No more summonings. He dies with this waking, permanently.

θ — Release the chamber figure: The sleeping core wakes fully, merging with the walking hand. The Witness becomes the full being. The god in flesh, loose.

ι — Return to the chamber voluntarily: The walking Witness descends, lies down beside the sleeping core, sleeps. Accepts being the tool forever.

See Part Nine for full ending details.

## PART TWO — THE ORDER

### Core Frame

The Witness Order is a fanatical religious cult disguised as a politically-neutral Concord administration institution. The public believes it administers the Concord plates, adjudicates disputes, trains the Witnesses. The private truth is that it is a cult, it worships the Black Star, and it exists to serve the Witness's cycle of summoning and containment.

### The Order's Founding Purpose (v8.1 correction)

v8 had the Order as a religious movement that formalized during iteration two's rebellion. v8.1 refines this.

The Order's proto-form predates iteration two. It predates the current Vesh dynasty. It grew up around the first Witness during his original walking and his early sleeps, as the coalition of priests, theologians, and political operators who had observed what he became during god-in-flesh periods and decided he had to be contained as well as served. By the time of iteration two, the Order existed as a functional institution; it formalized publicly as a state institution during iteration two's rebellion, but its private theological and operational structures were already old.

The Order and the current Vesh dynasty rose together as a public political arrangement. The Order itself, as a private priesthood, is older than the Vesh.

### The Order's True Function

The Order exists to:

1. Maintain the Witness's cycle. Keep the chamber figure safe. Perform summoning rites when needed. Prepare bodies, identities, and cover stories for each walking. Maintain the Long Record.

2. Contain the chamber figure. This is the part the public does not know and that most Order members do not know. Tier 4 knowledge. The Order's priesthood is also a prison-keeping function. The rites that summon the Witness are also the rites that ensure he returns to the chamber rather than walking free. The Long Record is an instrument of this containment as much as a memory-keeping device.

3. Preserve the host bloodline. Kill Touched children. Cull dangerous mutations. Manage marriages. Forge documents at every level up to the Concord plates themselves. Maintain the Vesh line's stability as the throne-host.

4. Enforce the Concord. Project authority across the galaxy through the Witness and through lower enforcement classes.

5. Prepare for cycle turns. Maintain readiness for future rituals.

## PART THREE — THE VESH AND THE CYCLE

### The Black Star

A god. Specifically, the god of successful rebellions — the cycle of rebellion-and-power itself, in some sense *is* the god. Its true name, form, and being are deferred.

The Black Star is older than the first Witness. It has been running the cycle across eternities through many vessels and arrangements before the Witness-form existed. What distinguishes the current era is that the Black Star's work has been institutionalized — there is now an Order around the vessel, a priesthood around the vessel, a dynasty arrangement, a codified cycle. Before, the god's work was less structured. The vessels were different shapes, the acts had different forms, the priests (if they existed) were different priests.

The Black Star operates through two institutionalized channels in the current era:

Throne-host channel — the ruling bloodline. Currently the Vesh. Every Vesh emperor has been a partial throne-host.

Priest-host channel — the Witness. Both states (chamber figure and walking form).

Neither channel works without the other in the current arrangement. The dynasty rules because the god gives them the Gift through the line. The Order preserves the dynasty because the god operates through its priest-host to keep the political-host stable.

The Black Star manifests visually in night skies as iteration three approaches. Early game: barely visible, slight distortion in the starfield. Approaching the ritual: visibly pulsing, growing, present. In the hidden apocalyptic endings: fills the sky; walks.

### The Gift, Cyclical Pattern, Iterations, Current Dynasty (Vesh), Dying Emperor Auriel

Preserved from v8 with one addition.

v8 iterations stand as written. Iteration zero remains deliberately unknowable. Iteration one is the first Witness's first walking — the transition from the Ruling Before to the first recorded dynasty. Iteration two is the transition from that dynasty to the current Vesh, including Miriath's killing. Iteration three is the present.

The addition: the Black Star's work predates iteration one. Whatever the god did before the first Witness is genuinely out of reach. The cycle as an institutionalized form begins with iteration one. What ran before was the same god in looser shape.

Auriel Vesh material preserved from v8. Emperor with fragmentary prescience. Poisoned by Sevasti at his request. Prepared Elara specifically to empower the Witness. Knew the Fourth would engineer the next summoning. Knew Elara would be the intended sacrifice. His fragmentary prescience is now explicitly fragmentary — he built plans around the fragments he had, and some of his predictions were wrong. Not all of his instructions in Elara's letters and testament are correct. A player may find an Auriel letter whose advice, followed, would harm them. Not because he betrayed them — because he was guessing, dying, working with partial vision.

The Penultima is cut (preserved from v6). The "dimming stars" lore is cut (preserved from v6). Replaced with the Black Star's growing visual manifestation.

### Miriath and the Template of the Ninth

New material in v8.1.

Miriath was the last heir of the first recorded dynasty. The first Witness killed her in iteration two's ritual climax. The killing was the ninth component of that iteration's ritual — eight factions' ratifications or relics plus Miriath's blood.

Miriath was Resonant Gifted. Her death bridged the Black Star's two host channels at the moment of her ending, which is what made iteration two's ritual turn cleanly. Without a Resonant Gifted at the bridging moment, the ritual does not complete in the form needed for stable transition.

Elara is Resonant Gifted. She is the current era's equivalent of Miriath. This is not coincidence. The Black Star's cycle produces a Resonant Gifted in the host bloodline at each ritual-ripe moment because the cycle requires one. Auriel, with his fragmentary prescience, understood this. He knew Elara was the one. He prepared her.

This tightens the cycle's mechanics: the ritual is not an arbitrary sacrifice, it is a structurally specific one requiring a figure of a specific kind. That figure is produced by the cycle itself, at the right moment, through the bloodline. Elara and Miriath are structurally the same person across iterations, at the level of function.

The current Witness's original deed-relics from his iteration-two walking include relics from Miriath's killing itself (what object was used, what was left behind). This is the ninth in the public record of his eight-plus-one acts — the acts of founding atrocity plus the climactic kill. In the current game, Elara's potential death parallels Miriath's structurally. The chamber meeting, for the hunter, resolves these into the same shape.

## PART FOUR — THE WORLD

### The Concord, Argent Court, Verge

Preserved from v8.

### The Nine Claimant Realms — v8.1 structure

Each claimant holds a planet cluster. Each cluster is its own zone narratively. Each is Bloodborne-adjacent for beasts and environmental horror.

v8.1 changes to the claimant-realm architecture:

Every claim has both a political claimant and a Vesh heir at its center. The claimant-heir relationship varies per realm. Some realms, claimant and heir are distinct (Damar and his disfigured cousin, the Glass Empress and her granddaughter, Liana and her ninth child). Some realms, the claimant is the heir (Nadezh is Vesh, is the claim). Some realms, the heir is functionally the claimant even with a nominal political operator (the Sevened's Vesh senator as living archive, with Ise Varn as operator). This varies by realm, designed per-realm.

Elara is the one Vesh heir not held by a claimant. Auriel hid her. The Witness retrieves her in the opening.

### The World Structure — v8.1 locks

The game is open-world after the opening.

The opening is authored sequence: Witness wakes in cabin, receives Sevasti's letter, travels to Nadezh's Verge orphanage, retrieves Elara. This establishes the ship, Elara, the Long Record, the ritual setup. Nadezh as a claimant is *met* but her claim is not resolved — she is alive and known to the Witness, a woman he cannot easily kill, but her political claim remains in play for later if the player's ending requires dealing with it.

After the opening, the galaxy opens. Seven claimant realms (plus Nadezh's, if and when the player returns to it politically) are available in any order. Player picks.

Difficulty is communicated diegetically through combat feedback. Enemies in higher-tier realms hit harder and resist more. A player walking into Kroft's territory early takes heavy damage quickly and either dies, retreats, or skill-substitutes through the difficulty. The world does not gate the player; the player gates themselves based on capability.

Realms have rough tiers (one through four, with one or two realms per tier), but the player never sees tier labels. The combat does the teaching.

### Aftermath Layer (NEW in v8.1)

The game carries a systematic aftermath layer. What the player does in a realm — whether they killed the claimant, spared them, allied with them, killed the heir, spared the heir — produces consequences that the player learns about later through multiple channels:

Bridge console news: the ship's bridge has a news feed the player can check between realms. It surfaces events from realms the player has previously visited, including aftermath of the player's actions. Some feeds are factual; some are distorted by galactic politics.

Letters: correspondence arrives at the cabin desk from various NPCs — Sevasti, Ise Varn, surviving claimants, unexpected senders. These reference aftermath.

Dialogue on later realms: NPCs in realms the player hasn't visited yet reference events in realms the player has. "You killed Damar, didn't you? I heard his cousin was executed by his own army a week later."

Long Record entries: when the player rests or writes, entries surface describing what the Witness has learned about realms he's left behind.

Aftermath authoring is staged. Three or four claimants get specific authored aftermath content — full narrative consequences that play out over subsequent realms, with specific NPCs reacting, specific events occurring, specific Long Record material. The remaining claimants carry generic-but-present aftermath acknowledgment — the game notes what the player did, but the consequences are lighter, more abstract.

Which three or four claimants get full aftermath treatment is a workshop item, to be decided when the eight deed-relic-power sets are workshopped. Likely candidates based on current design weight: Damar Hul, Liana Arvaux, Nadezh (if the player deals with her politically), the Sevened.

Aftermath triggers are tied to the game-clock (realms completed, rest periods, Long Record entries) rather than sequence position, because sequence is player-chosen.

## PART FIVE — ELARA AND THE PROGRESSION SYSTEM

### Who Elara Is

Preserved from v8. Eleven at game start. Resonant Gifted. Auriel's granddaughter, hidden on the Verge, prepared by him specifically to empower the Witness. Does not fight.

### The Witness-Elara-Relic Loop (NEW in v8.1 — primary progression mechanic)

The Witness's powers come through a three-part loop involving Elara, the eight claimant realms, and the relics recovered from each.

Step 1: The Witness visits a claimant realm. At the realm's structural end — typically a boss encounter that may or may not be the claimant directly, but yields the relic — he recovers a physical object. This object is a relic in the technical sense: it was present at, used in, or produced by one of the first Witness's original deeds, centuries or millennia ago. It has been preserved by the claimant's faction across generations, often without the current holders knowing what it really is. To most of the galaxy it looks like state regalia, a cursed heirloom, a sacred object of unclear origin, or an unremarkable item kept in a reliquary for reasons no one remembers.

Step 2: The Witness returns to the ship. He brings the relic to Elara.

Step 3: Elara performs an act of opening on the relic. Her Resonant Gift touches the trace of the Black Star's original channeling that the relic carries. She does not consume the relic; she opens it. The relic remains intact. What she extracts is the method — the technique, the ability, the remembered way of acting that the first Witness used when he produced the object. She gives this ability to the current Witness. Where there was one power in his kit, there are now two.

The relic, after opening, goes on display in the ship's cabin. Over the course of the game the cabin accumulates eight opened relics, each a quiet reminder of where a power came from. A player who pays attention to the cabin sees it filling. A player who does not pay attention has a workspace slowly becoming a reliquary without noticing.

The first blood-taking ritual (preserved from v8) establishes the bridge between Vesh and Witness that makes the relic-opening possible at all. That first ritual is an authored story beat early in the game, probably triggered shortly after Elara comes aboard. From that point, subsequent openings follow the relic-loop pattern.

### Powers — Two Registers

The first Witness was both institution-builder and god-in-flesh across his original walking. The powers Elara opens from his relics reflect both registers.

Founding-act powers: authority, naming, documentary cruelty, social unbecoming. The Long List ability (name a target publicly; named targets take more damage, NPCs will not help or defend them, the naming lasts until death or renaming). The Mark of Unbecoming (mark a target and observers gradually forget who they were, refusing to recognize or obey them). The Record's Authority (speak a ruling; the world briefly rearranges to confirm it, within narrow institutional contexts). These powers are sinister in specific institutional ways. They make the world worse by making the Witness's authority more effective.

Channeling-act powers: direct combat force. The fire the first Witness carried during god-in-flesh periods. The overwhelming strikes he delivered when the Black Star was burning through him. These scale as conventional combat upgrades — more damage, more range, more devastating execution attacks.

Rough mix: four founding-act, four channeling-act across the eight. Specific assignments per realm — which deed, which relic, which power register — is a workshop item. Preliminary guesses (to be revisited during workshop):

- Damar Hul / Thorned Provinces: channeling-act relic (war-era object, combat power).
- Liana Arvaux / Perfected Coast: founding-act relic (bloodline-imposition record, social/authority power).
- Aldric / Dreaming realm: founding-act relic (original dream-binding, prescience-related power).
- Glass Empress / court realm: founding-act relic (documentary or ceremonial, authority power).
- Kroft / Iron Parochy: channeling-act relic (raw-power artifact, devastating combat power).
- Nadezh / Verge: channeling-act relic (quiet, sleepless-hybrid-resonance power, possibly an endurance or awareness power).
- The Sevened / Senate Stars: founding-act relic (constitutional original, authority power).
- The Hollow / Orphan Worlds: channeling-act relic (god-in-flesh-era object, combat power).
- Ninth claimant (TBD): workshop-pending.

Eight relics. Eight powers. Plus any initial-kit powers the Witness starts with (short sword, bow, basic dodge — no relic-derived abilities).

The specific five-to-eight powers with full design is a workshop item. See Part Fourteen.

### The Memory-Return Layer (NEW in v8.1 — hunter-only)

Retrieving and opening a relic yields the power for every player. The memory-return is a separate, gated layer available only to players who engage with the realm deeply enough to trigger it.

In each realm, an environmental trigger exists that "wakes" the relic's history. The trigger varies by realm: standing in the specific ruin where the original act was committed; reading a specific inscription in a library; hearing an old NPC say a specific sentence; pressing the right stone; finding a preserved record in an out-of-the-way room. The trigger is not flagged by quest markers. The player either notices or does not.

If the trigger fires: when Elara opens the relic, a Long Record entry appears in the Witness's own hand — from centuries ago, in a voice he does not remember being — describing the deed that produced the relic. The current Witness reads the entry. He does not know, yet, that it is his own hand. But something in the body recognizes.

If the trigger does not fire: Elara opens the relic, the Witness gets the power, no entry surfaces.

A hunter who catches four or five triggers across the eight realms assembles a partial picture. A hunter who catches all eight has the full portrait of the first Witness's acts and, in combination with other channels (Ioran, the chamber figure, environmental storytelling, NPC recognition), reaches the chamber-meeting revelation with full understanding.

A non-hunter gets eight powers and a growing collection of opened relics in their cabin and no entries. They finish the game with an unexplained set of cool objects and a full combat toolkit.

### Elara's Non-Combat Role — Companion Witness

Preserved from v8: Elara does not fight. Not ever. She is on the ship, in the cabin, at rest.

New in v8.1: Elara witnesses the kills. Through presence on the ship, through the Long Record entries she reads, through the Witness's own returning from each realm with blood on his hands or without, she knows what he did. She reacts.

Her relationship to the Witness is authored to shift based on which specific kills he makes and under what justifications. A player who kills Nadezh (to pursue an ending that requires Nadezh's claim ended) loses something with Elara that cannot be fully recovered. A player who spares the Glass Empress when Elara thought she should have been ended gets Elara's confusion, her withdrawal, her questioning.

Elara's reactions are not a morality meter. They are authored content — specific dialogue, specific Long Record entries written in her hand, specific silences — that accumulate. By endgame, Elara's understanding of who the Witness is has been shaped by his specific record of kills and sparings. Her final scenes, and particularly her scenes in Ending ζ and the hidden endings, carry her full reading of him.

This is load-bearing authoring. Each potential kill needs Elara-reactions authored. Each potential sparing needs Elara-reactions authored. The GRRM register requires that these reactions not be morally proportional — sometimes Elara is more troubled by a sparing than a kill, sometimes her grief over a specific death is bigger than the player expected, sometimes her approval of a difficult choice is unexpectedly warm.

### The Blood-Taking Ritual (preserved from v8 with clarified role)

The ritual as described in v8 still happens. It functions as the bridge-establishment between Vesh and Witness that makes the relic-opening possible. The first blood-taking is early-game, post-Elara-retrieval. It's the mechanical unlock for the relic system.

Subsequent blood-takings may occur as story beats (the ritual is also how Elara reads the Long Record alongside the Witness, how she glimpses prior wakings of his body). Whether blood-taking is performed separately from relic-opening or integrated into the opening act is a design detail — workshop pending. Simplest reading: one foundational blood-taking scene establishes the bridge, and from that point the bridge is permanent; relic-openings draw on the established bridge without requiring repeated blood-taking.

### What Elara Knows — and Does Not

Elara knows she is Vesh. Knows she is Resonant Gifted. Knows Auriel prepared her for the Witness. Knows the Fourth want her as the ritual's final component. Knows the galaxy's math — that completing the ritual would be the outcome the Fourth's arithmetic calls best.

Elara does not know, at game start, that the current Witness is the chamber figure — the first Witness, the man whose deeds are public history. She learns this over the course of the game, alongside the player, if the player pursues the hunter's path. Auriel did not tell her this directly in his letters — his prescience was fragmentary and he did not know which waking would come for her. He prepared her for the kind of being she would travel with, not for the specific truth of who the Witness is.

This means Elara and the Witness discover together, through the ritual's progression and the memory-returns, what he is. This shared discovery is one of the game's most intimate dynamics, for players who reach it.

## PART SIX — THE NINE CLAIMANTS (v8.1 updated structure)

Each realm is a place, not a boss arena. Each is Bloodborne-adjacent: the player travels through, fights the horrors that claimant and their allies have produced or tolerated, and navigates the political-moral question at the realm's center.

Claimant-heir relationships vary per realm. Boss fights yield relics but are not the only path through the realm (see below — ending-specific kill requirements).

### Ending-Specific Kill Requirements (NEW in v8.1)

Reaching a given surface ending requires specific claimants to be killed and/or specific claimants to be spared. Example shapes (to be finalized during workshop):

- Ending α (name Elara): requires certain claimants whose claims would block Elara's legitimacy to be ended. Not all eight. A specific subset.
- Ending β (name Nadezh): requires claimants whose claims conflict with Nadezh's to be ended. Does not require Nadezh to be killed (she is the chosen emperor). May require the player to kill other claimants they respected.
- Ending γ (dissolve with Sevened): requires claimants whose claims conflict with constitutional dissolution to be ended. Likely requires Nadezh's death if the player has been sheltering her — the Sevened cannot operate with a living throne-claimant of her weight in play.
- Ending δ (burn the Record): no specific kill requirements beyond what the player chose along the way. This ending is about institutional destruction, not political succession.
- Ending ε (take the throne): requires the Witness to have demonstrated sufficient authority through kills and judgments. Specific kill set shapes the legitimacy he claims.
- Ending ζ (the sacrifice — hidden): requires all eight claimants killed plus Elara. The ritual path. The full first-Witness toolkit acquired. The hardest path mechanically and emotionally.

These requirements are not shown as a checklist in the UI. They emerge through the game's political logic — who's alive controls what's possible, the player infers constraints from dialogue and news, and at each ending's approach the requirements crystallize into the specific acts the ending needs.

Some players will reach endgame with the wrong kill-set for any clean ending and will have to make choices about which ending to pursue with what they have. Some endings may become unavailable mid-game because of earlier choices. This is correct.

### Bloodborne-Tier Beasts

Each realm has beasts shaped by the realm's heir and the Order's complicity. The beasts populate the realm for combat progression regardless of how the player resolves the claimant. Killing the claimant is one encounter; the realm's combat content is broader.

### Boss-Fight Structure

Boss fights yield relics. Specific boss design varies per realm — claimant alone, heir alone, both together, or an emergent confrontation shape per the realm's political structure. The player who wants the relic has to engage the boss encounter, which typically means kill. Some realms may permit alternate forms of engagement — stealth, negotiation, third-party intervention — but these are exceptions, not the default.

## PART SEVEN — CHARACTERS

### The Chamber Figure (v8.1 expanded)

The first Witness. The first being the Black Star worked through in Witness-form. Sealed in his chamber beneath Tessarene. Bound there partly as vessel, partly as punishment. Same being as the player.

His original walking produced eight public-history atrocities (the deeds described in Part One). His consent to becoming the Black Star's vessel happened before the deeds — he agreed to this, originally, for reasons that have not survived into the present even in his own memory. The sleep has eroded his own biography.

He is, now:
- Bodily preserved, physically changed through centuries of intermittent presence and the cost of carrying the Black Star's direct hosting.
- Mentally distributed through the signet.
- Temporally unstuck in minor ways.
- Small. Still. Blasphemous/ASOIAF register.
- Not fully aware of his walking self day-to-day. When the walking Witness holds the signet or enters specific sacred spaces, the chamber figure's awareness may briefly touch the walking self. But in general, the two states are not co-conscious. The chamber figure sleeps through the walking Witness's adventures.

The chamber meeting — in the hidden-ending path — happens when the walking Witness physically descends into the chamber. At that point the chamber figure opens his eyes. The two states are present in the same room, for the first time in the current waking. The walking Witness sees his own face.

Emotional register of the meeting: not a single register. A dialogue. Possibly the player's dialogue choices shape what the meeting becomes. Horror, kinship, grief, rage at him for what he did knowing he is you — these are possible emotional shapes, none is the only shape. The scene is authored as a conversation between the walking self and the sleeping self, both of whom are the player, with the player choosing which of them to privilege in the exchange.

His pre-original-walking identity — who he was before the Black Star took him — is deferred. My strong instinct: defer this permanently. The answer is not recoverable even to himself. The mystery is structural, not workshop-pending.

## PART EIGHT — THE LONG RECORD
