# BLACK STAR — World Bible v5
*Codename: The Ninth Heir*
*Snapshot: 2026-04-20*

---

## Writer's Intent (revised)

This is an anti-hero epic. The player is not a hero. The player is the latest in a nine-century chain of Witnesses who have quietly shaped the galaxy from behind a mask of neutrality, each doing what they believed was right, each making the next Witness's world worse. The Order is the villain. The bloodline it has preserved is the villain. The player, inheriting both, is the villain.

The story is told in the tradition of **GRRM** (political texture, no pure sides), **Tolkien** (deep time, names of things long gone), **Lovecraft** (knowledge as contamination), **Dune** (bloodline as weapon, prescience as curse), **Attack on Titan** (hero-becomes-monster, generational inherited sin), and **Gurren Lagann** (the one who crosses the Delaware — spirals of defiance, even unto ruin).

At the end of a full playthrough, we want the credits to land like a weight on the chest. The player has won nothing clean. They have chosen what to be complicit in. The second run is where the story opens.

---

## I. The Setting — The Concord, the Vesh, and the Order

The galaxy has been unified for seven centuries under the **House of Vesh**, governed through nine covenants called the **Concord** chained as steel plates to an orbital throne-ship, the **Argent Court**, near the galactic core.

Eleven years ago, Emperor **Auriel Vesh** died. He had no heirs he acknowledged. The reaffirmation went unsigned. The nine great bloodlines that signed the original Concord now each claim the throne.

The galaxy has not yet gone to war. Everyone is waiting to see who blinks.

The **Witness Order** was founded nine centuries ago to maintain the plates and adjudicate disputes. Witnesses have no formal political power. Their power is epistemic — when a Witness declares a fact about the Concord, no one has successfully contested it.

This is the story every child in the galaxy learns. It is a lie of omission. The Order has never been neutral. See Section V.

---

## II. The Pre-Vesh Era — The Thelenor Fall

Before the Vesh, the imperial line was the **Thelenor** — a dynasty older than recorded history, who ruled for eleven centuries because "they had always ruled." The Thelenor fell in a single year: a coordinated plague — or poisoning, no record is trustworthy — took the whole bloodline. Thirty-seven people in fourteen months. The last Thelenor, an eleven-year-old girl named **Miriath**, died in the Argent Court itself. Her body was never recovered.

The First Witness, **Sister Korien Vael**, was the woman who found Miriath dead. She was also, the player will eventually learn, the one who poisoned her.

Korien's reason was this: the Thelenor were *touched*. Their bloodline bore something — a mutation, a gift, a curse; the language is inadequate — that had grown across eleven centuries into something the galaxy could not survive. The last Thelenor generation was producing children who were not children. Miriath herself was still human. Her younger cousin was not. Korien killed Miriath to end the line before the non-human cousins could inherit.

The four years of chaos that followed, the years in which **Orin Vesh** rose, were not politics. They were Korien Vael manufacturing a new dynasty. She chose the Vesh — minor coreward nobility, clean blood, no taint — and she *made* them imperial. She assembled the nine bloodlines that would become the Concord signatories. She drafted the plates. She invented the ceremonial role of the Witness Order to monitor what she had just done, and then she swore her successors to forget.

The plates themselves contain her name nowhere. She destroyed every record of her involvement. The Order remembers her as "the First Witness," mother of the oath, moral exemplar. The truth is in a vault beneath the Order's motherhouse on the planet **Tessarene**. Your Witness will access that vault in the last act.

## III. The Vesh Bloodline — The Gift

Korien Vael did not save the galaxy. She traded it.

The Vesh line she elevated was clean, yes. But Korien was not just removing a dangerous gift from the imperial chair — she was putting a different one in its place. Orin Vesh had a cousin who could see the near-future. A sister who never aged. An uncle who could speak to the dead. The Vesh line, Korien decided, was a safer vessel for imperial-scale prescience than the Thelenor had become. Safer. Not clean.

For seven centuries, the Vesh line has been haunted by its gift. Most of them are born normal. A significant minority are not.

- **The Visible.** Born with visible anomaly. Extra vertebrae. Mirrored organs. Limbs that end wrong. Skin that responds to light. Historically these children have been killed at birth, quietly, with records falsified. The current living count of Visible Vesh in hidden asylums across the galaxy is four. The player can find them. They do not forgive what was done to them.
- **The Hybrid.** Born bearing traits of other species, other taxonomic kingdoms. A Vesh cousin in the 6th century who grew antlers. A Vesh duchess in the 9th who breathed underwater. The current generation: Nadezh Vol is one. She has not disclosed which trait. You can learn it. She will not like that you did.
- **The Gifted.** Born normal in appearance. Touched in other ways. Prescience. Memory perfection. The ability to charm, to persuade beyond natural limits. The ability to speak languages they have never learned. Auriel Vesh was Gifted. Elara is Gifted. Most of the nine claimants have Gifted ancestors in the last three generations.
- **The Touched.** Born with a specific and dangerous pattern that shows up once every three or four generations — a child that is, in Korien's old private language, "Thelenor-shaped." An ancestor's face. An ancestor's gifts. Sometimes an ancestor's goals. The Touched, when they appear, are killed. This has always been Witness Order work. Witnesses have murdered Vesh children for seven centuries to maintain the line's stability. Every Witness who has killed a Touched child has the record in their dossier.

Elara Vesh is Gifted. The child in Liana Arvaux's bio-lab — the ninth heir of the Perfected Coast — is Touched. This is why Liana is trying to breed her line perfectly: she has a Touched daughter she cannot bear to kill, and she is trying to out-breed the shape.

The bloodline is coming apart. The last three generations have produced unusually many Gifted and Touched. The Order's private projection is that the next generation — Elara's children, if she has them — will be mostly Hybrid or Touched. The line will collapse into the thing Korien killed Miriath to prevent.

This is the cosmic threat. Not a god. Not the Penultima. It is in the blood. It has been in the blood for nine centuries. The galaxy has been kept stable at the cost of a specific ongoing crime that the Witness Order has been committing for all nine of those centuries.

The stars are *not* dimming supernaturally. What the Verge people are calling the "dimming" is actually the visible failure of the Concord's quiet mechanisms — Vesh-gift-powered logistics, Vesh-gift-powered jump-route stability, Vesh-gift-powered communications. Auriel's death didn't just leave a political vacuum. It withdrew a technological substrate the galaxy had forgotten was being provided. The Verge is going dark because the distributed prescient network the Vesh line has quietly maintained for seven centuries is starving without its central node.

Elara, trained correctly and willing, could restore it. But she is eleven. And the training is not painless. And the Order intends to put her through it anyway.

## IV. The Emperor Auriel Vesh

Auriel was the forty-seventh Vesh Emperor. He reigned forty-one years. He was Gifted — specifically, he could see the approximate shapes of the near future, in fragments, in dreams. He married late and had no legitimate children. The reason was: he had seen his own children and could not bear to sire them.

In his last decade, Auriel did something no Vesh Emperor had done: he tried to end his own line.

He stopped attending court. He sealed his library. He funded Verge-edge expeditions that pretended to look for the Penultima (a myth he invented to distract the Keepers) while actually looking for every hidden Visible and Touched and Gifted Vesh cousin in the galaxy. He wrote to each of them. He offered them one of three things: quiet extraction to the Verge, honest conversation, or a clean death of their choosing.

He corresponded with three people at the end: **Mother Sevasti**, **Ser Nadezh Vol**, and **Elara Vesh**. He asked Sevasti to open the Order's oldest vault — the one under Tessarene — and read the truth of Korien Vael. She did. She has not yet told anyone what she read.

He asked Nadezh to take the throne briefly, sign one law that would dissolve the Concord, and then die cleanly. She refused. Not from cowardice. She told him: *this has to be done by someone who does not know what they are doing. It is the last gift you can give the galaxy.*

He asked Elara nothing. He only wrote to her. He told her everything he knew. He left her to decide.

Auriel was murdered. The physician of record, Elder Brother Imrys of the Keepers, was bribed to sign the "organ failure" finding. Auriel was poisoned by a Witness on Sevasti's order. Sevasti did it to fulfill a request Auriel had made of her personally: *when I cannot do it myself, you will do it. Do not let my Gift outlive my will.* Sevasti did her duty. It has broken her. She has spent eleven years trying to make it worth what she did.

## V. The Witness Order — Nine Centuries of Interventions

The Order's public history: neutral observers, keepers of the Concord, loyal only to truth.

The Order's real history: a covert political apparatus that has murdered Vesh children for nine centuries, assassinated heads of state an average of once per century, forged documents at every level up to and including the Concord plates themselves, and engineered at least four "natural" disasters to collapse states that threatened the stability of the line it was secretly preserving.

All of this is in the Order's private archives, organized by Witness. Each Witness who joins takes a second oath — never spoken aloud — to the **Long Record**. Witnesses read their predecessors' confessions. Witnesses write their own. A Witness does not die without her final testimony in the Long Record. Your dossier is not just a map of what you've seen — it is a confession waiting to be completed.

The Order has three active factions, and a fourth that it does not acknowledge exists.

**The Orthodox.** Believe the Long Record is necessary. The oath is intact. We have done what was required. We will do what is required. Led by **Elder Brother Vassen Kir**, a 78-year-old with beautiful manners who has personally killed thirteen Touched children in his career.

**The Prescient.** Led by **Mother Sevasti**. Believe the Order must prevent the collapse it sees coming, even if that means working against the Concord directly. Sevasti has been forging edicts under the dead claimant Andrei Serev's name for three years to prevent any claim from formalizing. She is also continuing — reluctantly — the killing of Touched children. She does not lie to herself about it.

**The Dissolved.** Small, heretical. Believe the Order itself is the ongoing crime and must end. Believe Witnesses should be free political actors, accountable only to their own consciences. Some have defected to the Sevened. Led, if led is the word, by **Sister Olen Rho**, a former Prescient who crossed over when she could no longer bear the killings.

**The Fourth.** Not acknowledged. Not officially real. A thread within the Order that is loyal not to the Concord but to Korien Vael personally — to her original logic, to her willingness to do the unforgivable for the sake of continuity. They believe Elara should be killed now, before she is trained. They believe the line should be ended cleanly by Order hand, as Korien ended the Thelenor. They have attempted this once already in the game's backstory. They will try again. The player may or may not identify them in time.

Your Witness's faction is randomized within the legacy mechanic, and each faction faction unlocks different routes. But the player will also learn, over time, that *they* are not automatically loyal to the faction their character was trained into. They can defect. Defection has consequences.

## VI. Previous Witnesses — The Dossier

The Order's Long Record is in-game content. Each playthrough, the player can read fragments of it in the Order's motherhouse library or in *Impartial Regard*'s cabin safe. The fragments are procedurally selected from a large authored pool — some always appear; many are gated by what the player has already done.

The most consequential previous Witnesses, all of whom the player will learn about:

**Sister Korien Vael (First Witness, 9 centuries ago).** Poisoner of Miriath Thelenor. Founder of the Order. The original crime.

**Brother Thavian Ost (4 centuries ago).** Forged three Concord plate signatures during the Mad Years when two claimants went to war over a dead emperor. The forgeries held. The war ended. His Long Record confession runs 40 pages. The plates still bear his forgery. Nobody else knows.

**Sister Veyla Mir (2 centuries ago).** Assassinated a reigning Vesh empress — Auriel's great-grandmother — who had decided to publicly disclose the Long Record. Veyla strangled her in her bath. Her confession is one paragraph. The empress was, by Veyla's own account, correct to want disclosure.

**Brother Ioran Kest (43 years ago).** Disappeared mid-mission. No confession in the Long Record. The Order considers his file permanently open. In fact, Ioran Kest did not disappear — he *defected* to the Fourth faction and then, horrified by what he learned about himself, walked away from everything. He is alive on a Verge moon. He is the reason Nadezh renounced her claim: he found her as a girl, told her the whole truth, and let her decide. He is also the reason Damar Hul carved his skin: Ioran was there for the end of the Crown Wars and he witnessed Damar's crime and did not stop it and did not report it, choosing instead to let Damar live with it. Ioran is the ghost in every corner of the game. He has a questline. It is difficult to find. He does not want to be found.

**Sister Adhvi Ren (your most immediate predecessor).** Died eleven months ago, murdered in a Keeper compound on the Reliquary. Her confession is incomplete — she was writing it when she died, and the pages are torn. What survives is twelve sentences. The player will read them on first login. They will not make sense. They will make sense later. The Order does not know who killed her. The player can discover it. The killer is: Elder Brother Imrys, who thought she had found Auriel's real cause of death. He was right that she had.

Every planet the player visits has the fingerprints of previous Witnesses. Completing a claimant's dungeon reveals what the Order has done in that cluster over centuries, and the player, eventually, has the choice to continue the intervention or to undo it. Either choice is expensive. Some worlds will literally fall apart if the player undoes decades-old Order work. Others will find peace they haven't known in a generation. The game does not tell the player which is which in advance.

## VII. The Ship — *Impartial Regard*

Small, old, unfashionable. Forty-three Witnesses have sailed it. Forty-one died aboard. It bears the Order signet. It cannot carry heavy weaponry.

New in v3: the ship has a **cabin safe**. Inside: the current Witness's partial dossier, *the last three pages of Sister Adhvi Ren's confession torn out of sequence*, and a locked drawer the Witness does not have the key to. The key is found in the third act of the main story, or earlier through Ioran Kest's questline. Inside the locked drawer: a small wooden box containing six vials of an Order-made poison used for ending Touched children, and a handwritten list in Auriel Vesh's hand of eleven names. Three of those names are claimants. One of them is Elara.

The ship carries ghosts. The player will hear them, late in the game, when running low on sanity resources. They will say specific things from specific Witnesses' confessions. They will ask the player questions they cannot answer.

The ship cannot be replaced. If destroyed — which a small number of bad outcomes permit — the run ends. The Order does not lend a second ship to the same generation.

## VIII. Companions

Three companions can accompany the Witness for stretches of the game. None of them fight. All of them are consequential. Each has their own moral gravity and can die.

**Meridian.** An ex-soldier from the Crown Wars, mid-40s, rough and sober. Served under Damar Hul. Deserted after the atrocity Damar committed — she was the one who opened the gates of the burning city to let civilians out. She is on the Witness's ship because Sevasti sent her: Meridian knows the Crown Wars in a way the Order's records do not, and Sevasti needed a ground-level truth. Meridian's arc: she will discover, mid-game, that Ioran Kest was there. That he watched and did not stop it. If the player pursues Ioran's questline, Meridian will demand a confrontation. The confrontation has three outcomes, none clean.

**Vaspar.** A Perfected Coast defector. Early 30s. Soft-spoken. Was being groomed as Liana Arvaux's ninth heir's tutor before he escaped. He knows the bio-lab from the inside. He is not what he appears. He is the sixth heir of the program — an earlier attempt, judged inadequate and released to live privately, whose intelligence was used for decades to refine the later generations. He thinks of himself as flawed. He is, by any reasonable measure, extraordinary. If the player learns this and tells him, he breaks — in a way that either opens the deepest version of his questline or ends it in despair. If the player does not tell him, he helps sabotage Liana's program and leaves the ship at mid-game believing he was disposable, still.

**Kestrel.** A Dissolved Witness. Younger than you. Smart. Angry. Defected because she was asked to kill a Touched infant and refused. She came aboard *Impartial Regard* as a stowaway in the first act and you can either turn her in to Sevasti — clean, career-positive, and Kestrel is executed — or shelter her. Sheltering her makes her your most reliable ally and opens Sister Olen Rho's entire questline. It also means that if Sevasti ever discovers Kestrel aboard, the ship is recalled, the run ends badly, and the next Witness's faction is forced to Orthodox with reduced trust.

In addition to the three companions, **Elara Vesh** is a permanent quasi-companion across the arc of the game. She does not travel with the ship — she is tied to the Nursery — but she writes to the Witness, and late in the game, she can be extracted and brought aboard. Her presence on *Impartial Regard* changes everything, including which endings are available. She is the only child character in the game who speaks honestly about what she is.

## IX. The Nine Claimants (v3 — bloodline integrated)

Each cluster contains 1–2 hand-crafted planet dungeons. Each claimant is a final boss or the reason one exists.

**1. Damar Hul — The Penitent King, Thorned Provinces.** Carves prayer-sigils into his skin as penance for Crown Wars atrocities. He is Gifted: pain feedback. He *feels* the suffering of anyone within his sphere of influence. This is why his reign is stable — he cannot permit abuses because he physically feels them. His penance is not self-flagellation; it is a homeopathic dose he administers to maintain control over a power that would otherwise drive him mad. The player can help him refine the practice — opening a redemption arc. Or the player can interrupt it, which drives him into the state he is terrified of: he loses control and *commands* through pain. The Thorned Provinces become a theocracy of shared agony in a matter of weeks.

**2. Liana Arvaux — The Glass Empress, Perfected Coast.** Her line has been breeding for Giftedness without Touched expression for four generations. Her daughter, the ninth heir, is Touched. Liana will not kill her. She is trying to out-breed the shape: the tenth heir, being gestated now, may or may not be clean. Liana knows the Order kills Touched children. Liana is preparing to fight the Order. She will ask the Witness to join her, or to leave the Coast entirely. Neutrality is not available. If the player helps Liana, the tenth heir survives, and the Touched ninth heir lives her whole life in a medical pod — beautiful, not-quite-human, alive. If the player reports Liana to the Order, both daughters die, and Liana is executed. If the player kills the ninth heir personally at Liana's request — Liana cannot do it herself — the Perfected Coast's line ends clean. Liana will never speak to the Witness again. She will survive. The Coast will dim.

**3. Aldric, Sixth House — The Dreaming Patriarch, Dreaming Reach.** Two hundred years old. Gifted: temporal dislocation. He lives in multiple times simultaneously. The catatonia is deliberate — moving through the "waking" layer while his body is seventy years old almost killed him. He is now conserved. He can be woken; the instructions are hidden in his grandchildren's private archive. Waking him costs him fifteen more years of remaining life and the legitimacy of his grandchildren, who have ruled competently. Awake, he is the only claimant who can see the bloodline crisis coming from all sides at once. He can potentially negotiate with the Order's Fourth faction to stop the Touched killings going forward — he is the only person old enough to know their full internal history. Waking him also means he will die before the game ends. The grandchildren know this. They have prepared.

**4. Vassili Kroft — The Iron Surgeon, Iron Parochy.** Believes legitimate Vesh blood can be reconstructed from fragments scattered through the commoner population. He is correct, technically. He is also a fanatic. His laboratory is a dungeon full of children preserved in various states of surgical completion. He will release them if the player argues a better use. The argument Kroft respects is: "You are doing what Korien Vael did, and you are doing it worse." Kroft does not know about Korien — he will demand the player prove the claim. If the player can produce evidence from the Tessarene vault, Kroft will stop the program and dismantle his laboratory. He will also, quietly, submit himself to Order judgment for the children he has already destroyed. He expects to die for it. He will die for it. Whether the player brings him the evidence is a choice about whether they are willing to collapse the Iron Parochy entirely — because Kroft's successor has no such conscience.

**5. The Seven Keepers — The Devout, The Reliquary.** They have Auriel's preserved heart in their orbital cathedral. They believe he will return. They are correct that they have his heart. They are wrong about what that means. Auriel had his heart preserved on purpose: it carries a specific imprint of his Gift that can be used, with extreme difficulty, to "call down" his prescient abilities one final time. The Keepers do not know how to do this. Elder Brother Imrys — murderer of Sister Adhvi Ren — does. If the player unites with the Keepers, they gain access to one decisive prescient vision late in the game that answers one question of the player's choosing, honestly and completely. The questions available are terrible. Choosing is a major plot beat. If the player destroys the Keepers, the heart is lost, the question cannot be asked, and several endings remain ambiguous the player could have resolved.

**6. Elara Vesh — The Child, The Nursery.** See Section X.

**7. Ser Nadezh Vol — The Exile, The Verge.** Auriel's great hope. She is Hybrid — her specific trait is that she does not sleep. She has been awake for fifty-one years. She has had five decades of unbroken thought to reach the conclusions she has reached. She runs the orphanage because the children there are, disproportionately, Verge-born Vesh descendants whose families did not know the connection. She hides them. The Order knows she does this. Sevasti has not acted on it. Nadezh will not meet the Witness until the Witness has earned the trust of three specific NPCs (one in each of three other clusters). The conversation between them is long. It is the most important dialogue in the game. She will offer the Witness the following: *I will take the throne if you ask me to. I will do it because you ask me. And I will make you complicit in what happens after.* Her ending, if named, is Ending B — and it is the only version of Ending B that the game depicts with any hope. It is also, by Nadezh's own assessment, a trap. She is not certain she can hold the Gift. She thinks there is a 40% chance she will become Touched under the throne's pressure. She has told Auriel's letter-pile the math. She tells the Witness the math. She asks them to choose.

**8. The Sevened — The New Concord, Senate Stars.** Their theorist Counselor Ise Varn has reasoned out 80% of the bloodline truth on her own. She knows there is a biological substrate to the line that is failing. She intends to end it by dissolving the institution. She will authorize assassinations to clear the way, yes — but she will first try diplomacy, and she is better at diplomacy than the Concord has ever given her credit for. Allied with Ise, the Witness can end the Concord without war, if Elara is willing to make public testimony. Elara's testimony will cost her her life, from the political reaction — not the Gift, not the Penultima, just human beings angry. The Sevened's offer is: a stable constitutional galaxy, purchased with Elara's specific death, executed cleanly. Ise Varn will tell the Witness this directly. She will not hide it.

**9. The Hollow — The Name of Serev, The Orphan Worlds.** Forgery run by Mother Sevasti for three years. The Orphan Worlds know. Specifically, they have always known. When Andrei Serev died, the ninth bloodline's surviving elders contacted Sevasti first — they needed the delay as badly as she did. The Orphan Worlds are the only cluster that has understood the full shape of the crisis since year zero. They are also the cluster most willing to *accept* an Ending D outcome — burning the record, letting the galaxy forget, dying quietly. If the player ends the Serev forgery before understanding this, the Orphan Worlds close to the Witness. Forever.

## X. Elara Vesh

Eleven years old at game start. By game end, fourteen or fifteen, if she lives.

Gifted. Specifically: she is a *resonant* Gifted — she can borrow, briefly, the gift of anyone whose blood she has tasted. This is rare even by Vesh standards. She has, in her short life, knowingly tasted the blood of three people: her mother (who died in childbirth, blood on the sheets — visionary, one week of dreams), her regent Elder Brother Imrys (who bandaged her cut hand when she was six — she now carries his capacity for emotional detachment), and Auriel Vesh himself (she traveled to him once, in secret, at age nine — he cut his own palm and offered it to her. She has his prescience, limited, in fragments, since.)

Auriel wrote to her for two years. The letters are in a hollow wall of the Nursery library. She has read them all. She understands. She has known, since she was nine years old, that she will almost certainly die before she is twenty. She has chosen how she wants it to happen: with dignity, on her terms, after meeting the Witness Auriel promised her.

She is not a victim. She is eleven years old and has spent two years preparing for her own death. She will test the Witness. She lies. She tells the truth. She is capable of extraordinary affection. She can die in many ways — poisoning, assassination, regent politics, an attempt to flee, the Order's Fourth faction, a failed coronation, Ise Varn's clean execution. Most of those deaths can be prevented. Some require the Witness to break every oath. The game does not tell the Witness which is which.

She is the only character in the game who speaks honestly about what the Vesh line is. She does not flinch from the Touched cousins. She has visited the hidden asylum on Mentiel and met her own Visible cousins. She is not afraid of them. She is afraid *for* them. She will, if the Witness earns her full trust, ask the Witness to do one specific thing: *when I die, whatever way I die, find them, and do not let the Order finish them.*

The hidden asylums, plural, are the endgame's quietest thread. The Order will, if the Vesh line ends, execute the remaining Visible and Hybrid Vesh to close the books. The Witness can prevent this. Most endings do not prevent this. One does.

## XI. The Mid-Game Turns

There are three. They are designed to hurt in different directions.

**First Turn — The Dossier (approx. 30% through).** The Witness is asked to complete their own Long Record entries. In the course of doing this, they discover a sealed sub-section: *Sister Adhvi Ren's unfinished confession.* Reading it reveals: Adhvi believed the Order was preparing to kill Elara. The pages end mid-sentence, as she describes being observed by someone in the Reliquary. The Witness now knows Adhvi was murdered, and suspects the killer was in the Order. They will suspect the wrong person first. They will be encouraged by another Witness (Brother Vassen Kir, Orthodox faction elder) to drop the investigation.

**Second Turn — The Vault (approx. 60% through).** The Witness accesses the Tessarene vault and reads the truth of Korien Vael. The moment is silent. There is no dramatic cutscene. The player sits and reads. After reading, the Order's entire game changes. Every previous Witness the player admired is recontextualized. Every current Order figure's motives are now ambiguous. Sevasti, after this point, will have one long conversation with the Witness, and will offer to resign her position — to step aside and let the Witness decide what the Order should be. She means it. She has been waiting eleven years for someone to reach this point.

**Third Turn — The Companion (varies, ~70-85% through, gated by which companion is closest to the Witness).** The companion the Witness has grown closest to is revealed to have been placed there deliberately. By whom, and to what purpose, varies:

- *Meridian* was placed by Sevasti, who knew Meridian would eventually be forced to confront Ioran Kest. Sevasti wanted Ioran found. She used Meridian as bait. Meridian, on learning this, will leave the ship. She may be reconciled later. She may not.
- *Vaspar* was placed by Liana Arvaux herself. He is a sleeper asset, not a traitor — Liana did not program him. She simply sent him where he was most likely to meet a Witness who might protect her daughters, and gave him the freedom to tell or not tell. If the Witness learns this, and does not tell Vaspar, the game judges the Witness.
- *Kestrel* was placed by the Fourth faction. They put her near the Witness to identify, through her movements, whether the Witness would be sympathetic to the argument that Elara should be killed. Kestrel herself does not know this. When the Fourth makes contact, Kestrel has to choose whether to betray the Witness to her original placement or to stand with them. She has one dialogue to decide. The dialogue is two pages long. The player has 30 seconds of real time to read and respond.

## XII. The Endings (v3)

Five available. All are complicity. None resolve cleanly. The Luke-burning-Vader sentiment you named is the north star.

### Ending α — **Name Elara.**
The Witness names Elara. She travels to the Argent Court under Witness escort. She refuses the throne publicly, in the ritual language of the Concord, in a ceremony the nine bloodlines must recognize. The line is broken by its own act. The Gift begins to dissipate from the galaxy — not all at once, across a generation. Verge worlds go fully dark within a year. Core worlds slowly, over decades. The galaxy becomes mundane. Millions die in transitional collapses. Many more survive. No more Touched children are born. No more Witnesses need to kill them. Elara lives. She is fifteen. She goes to the Verge with Nadezh. She will live to old age. The Order, without its secret mandate, fractures. The player's own Witness line ends within two generations. The Long Record is burned voluntarily.

*Epilogue:* A single long take of Elara, aged forty, in the Verge, walking a beach with the Witness's eventual last successor. Two silhouettes. No dialogue. A nebula burning out in the distance. The credits intercut with passages from Auriel's letters to her, read in his voice.

### Ending β — **Name Nadezh.**
The Witness asks Nadezh to take the throne. She accepts, because the Witness asked. She reigns. Her hybrid trait — sleeplessness — means she governs for twenty-three years before her mind breaks. She is, by every measure, the best emperor the galaxy has had since before the Thelenor fall. She also, near the end, becomes Touched — the gift pressure transforms her. In her final year she begins to give orders that make no sense, that gesture toward the Penultima myth Auriel invented, that describe futures no one else can see. She dies. The galaxy is stable, prosperous, and afraid. The Order, kept alive by Nadezh's rule, quietly continues killing Touched children. This was foreseen. The Witness agreed to it. Twenty-three years of stability for the cost of Nadezh's mind and the ongoing crime.

*Epilogue:* Nadezh, at the end, in the Argent Court, dictating a letter to her successor-designate that she has chosen herself. The successor is not a Vesh. The successor is a commoner child she found in her orphanage decades before. The letter does not say what the child is supposed to do. The credits roll over Nadezh's voice, slowing, confused.

### Ending γ — **Dissolve the Concord, with the Sevened.**
Ise Varn takes the Argent Court. Elara gives public testimony. Elara is murdered in her bed three weeks later by a mourning noble. The galaxy fragments into a hundred constitutional experiments. The Sevened becomes a central body among many, not a throne. Over two centuries, the galaxy's population triples, its cultural range explodes, and its military capacity collapses. One of the new polities will, in the 4th century after dissolution, rediscover the Vesh bloodline in a sequestered population and start breeding for the Gift again. The cycle begins again. The Witness does not live to see it. They die in bed, an old person, on the Verge. They have lived a quiet life. They believe, mostly, they did the right thing.

*Epilogue:* A montage of the two centuries after. Small human triumphs. Small human atrocities. A child, four centuries from now, displaying a Gifted trait. An older man watching her, with a familiar symbol on his sleeve — a new Witness Order, born again without knowing its history. Fade.

### Ending δ — **Burn the Record.**
The Witness destroys the Long Record. Personally. In the Tessarene vault. The Order, without its institutional memory, finds its purpose slipping within a generation. The Concord continues. The claimants continue their war-that-is-not-yet-a-war. Elara's fate depends on what the Witness told her before burning the record: if they warned her, she flees to the Verge and survives. If they did not, the Order's Fourth faction kills her within a year. Either way, the galaxy's political crisis continues, unresolved. The dimming does not stop. The Gift continues. The killings continue. But the lie is at least no longer archived. When the truth is eventually rediscovered, it will not be by the Order. That, the Witness decides, is the smallest and most honest victory available.

*Epilogue:* The Witness alone in the ashes of the Long Record, on a Tessarene balcony, looking at the stars. They recite a passage from Korien Vael's confession from memory — they read it before they burned it. The camera holds. Credits over candle smoke.

### Ending ε — **Take the Throne.** (Hidden, high requirements.)
Requirements: must have defected from your starting Witness faction at least once; must have sheltered Kestrel through the Third Turn; must have read the Tessarene vault and met Ioran Kest; must have earned Nadezh's full testimony and refused her offer; must have gotten Elara aboard *Impartial Regard* and extracted her to the Verge before the Argent Court summit; must hold the Concord access tokens from Sevasti.

The Witness goes to the Argent Court alone. Not to name an heir. Not to dissolve. To take the throne, as Korien Vael did not — openly.

The plates refuse the signing. The Witness is not Vesh. The plates know. The Witness does not sign with ink or blood or wax. They sign with the Order's signet, and with the Long Record itself, physically pressed into the plates. The plates accept the Order as the bloodline. The Order *is* the bloodline. It has been, for nine centuries.

The galaxy reorganizes. The Witness rules openly as the first explicitly acknowledged Order Emperor. The Gift is gone — it leaves the Vesh line the moment the plates accept the substitution. Elara lives. She is freed. She is also furious. She never forgives the Witness. She writes one letter. It does not arrive.

The Witness rules for forty years. They become something the galaxy recognizes as necessary and hates. The Order becomes the dynasty. The new oath that replaces the old is spoken aloud: *we have done what was required. We will do what is required. We will be known for what we are.* It is an honest oath. It is also a confession, made standing.

*Epilogue:* The Witness at 70, on the Argent Throne, alone at night. A child — a Witness trainee — enters. The Witness motions them forward. They hand the child a book: the new Long Record, Volume I. The child opens it to the first page. The Witness dictates the first entry. The entry begins: *"I was the one who crossed the Delaware."* Credits.

## XIII. The Long Record as Living Object

The Long Record is not a menu. It is a rendered physical book: leather-bound, worn, half-full, sitting on a small desk in the cabin of *Impartial Regard* and reproduced at every Witness sanctum on every planet. When the Witness opens it, the screen shows the book. Pages turn. Marks on the paper — sigils, blots, glued-in corrections, torn corners — persist across generations.

The Long Record replaces what would otherwise be four separate systems: the quest log, the lore codex, the save file, and the ending-flag display. All of it lives in the book.

### The Three Layers of Entries

**Your own entries.** After every significant event — a claimant meeting, a moral decision, a death, a discovered truth — the book opens and the Witness writes. The player does not type. They choose, from 2–4 authored phrasings, *how* the Witness will record the event. Each phrasing is a flag. Each flag is read by NPCs, by your successor, by the Order itself.

The phrasings are never "good" and "evil." They are tonal and specific. After meeting Damar Hul and hearing his confession, a player might choose between:
- *"He believes his pain has bought him the right to rule. I cannot say he is wrong."*
- *"A murderer who has learned to perform sorrow. I will not be deceived."*
- *"He is what the Order makes of men who live long enough to reflect."*
- *"I did not know what to write here. I have left the page for later."*

The fourth is a revisit flag — the only way to defer a judgment, and the book marks the page with a small folded corner the Witness can return to once. Once.

**Predecessors' entries.** Fragments you unlock. Some are open from day one — standard Order-approved excerpts the novitiate Witness is taught to read. Most are locked. Unlocks require specific in-world actions: recovering a signet, visiting the Tessarene vault, finding a language primer, earning a senior Witness's trust. Reading an unlocked predecessor entry may itself trigger a writing prompt — "What do you make of this?" — and the Witness must commit an opinion. Sevasti is watching. Your successor will read your opinion of Veyla Mir before they read Veyla Mir.

**Unfinished missions.** Every previous Witness's dossier contains work they did not finish. Not side quests — open field investigations interrupted by death, defection, or despair. Some can be completed. Some cannot. Completing them is not optional flavor — five of the six endings require at least one completed legacy mission, and the sixth ending requires completing a specific one (see Ending ζ below). Details of the five named missions in Section XVI.

### Commitment Model

Entries are Souls-style. Once written, they cannot be unwritten. The book will not permit an erasure. If the Witness attempts to tear a page out, the act is itself logged — the book notes which page was torn and when, and the next Witness sees the stub. Tearing is always a traceable escalation.

There is exactly one way to revise an entry: if the Witness used the fourth ("deferred") phrasing and folded the page corner, they may return once and write a real phrasing. Any other attempt to rewrite fails. The player will learn this early — on the second major decision — and the lesson is intended to teach the weight of writing.

### The Book as Save System

The Long Record is the save system. There are no other saves. When the Witness writes, the game saves. When the Witness does not write, the game does not save. This has real consequences:

- **Resting on the ship writes a brief status entry.** (*"I slept poorly. The dreams returned."*) This is the cheapest save. It does not record decisions, only presence. Free.
- **Writing at a Witness sanctum on a planet saves the full game state.** Sanctums are rare — one per cluster, sometimes two. Reaching one is its own small quest. Bosses and key moments are always between sanctums.
- **Death without a recent entry means the previous entry is what the next Witness inherits.** This is the core tension. If the Witness dies before writing about a decision, that decision is effectively undone for the successor — but the world-state (NPC deaths, broken things, lost chances) is not. The successor inherits the consequences without the context. They will have to reconstruct what happened. Sometimes they cannot.
- **Auto-save exists only for crash recovery.** A crash auto-save restores to the last committed entry. The game does not help a player who died before committing.

This means the player must learn to write *in advance of danger*. Before the boss. Before the confrontation. The ritual is Souls-like — approach the sanctum, write, then walk into what you have decided to walk into.

### The Living Book

The book's physical state evolves. Visible artifacts the player will see in their own generation and in the master copies preserved at Tessarene:

- **Seals** appear on pages where the Witness took a formal action — a judgment rendered, a claimant recognized, a death sentenced. Red wax. The player presses the Order signet on a warm wax blot using a held button.
- **Blots** appear where a previous Witness's hand shook. The game renders them. Veyla Mir's confession has a specific blot near the word *"strangled."* Adhvi Ren's twelve surviving sentences end in a blot that becomes a long diagonal smear where the pen dragged when she was killed.
- **Insertions** appear when later Witnesses disagreed with predecessors. Glued-in protests. Marginalia. The most recent insertion in Korien Vael's founding volume is a single sentence in Auriel Vesh's own handwriting, on a loose slip of paper Sevasti placed there eleven years ago: *"She did not have the right."* Auriel cannot have written this. Sevasti placed it. The player will find it before they know who wrote it. It will take them most of the game to understand why.
- **Torn corners** appear where pages have been removed. The Order's master copies have seven torn pages, each a different Witness, each for a different reason. Four of the seven can be recovered from various locations in the galaxy. One is in Ioran Kest's personal effects on his Verge moon. One was eaten by Veyla Mir before she died so it could not be read. One is in Elara's hollow wall, among Auriel's letters — it was torn out by Auriel himself. The player will want all four.

### The Bindery at Tessarene

The Order's master bindery is beneath the motherhouse on Tessarene. The master copies of every volume are here, kept by two elderly sisters whose names the player learns only if they speak gently. These two have read more of the Long Record than any living Witness. Neither is senior. Neither is consulted. They know everything.

The player can, through a specific late-game quest, commission repairs to master volumes — gluing in found pages, pressing forgotten seals, binding new signatures. These additions are visible to all future Witnesses and affect legacy-generation starting conditions for future Witnesses the Order dispatches after the player's current death.

One commission is irreversible and game-critical: the new Long Record that begins after Korien Vael's unfinished final volume. Writing the first entry in that empty volume is the act that commits Ending ζ (see Section XVI).

## XIV. Unfinished Missions

Each named previous Witness has an active case in the current timeline that their death or defection interrupted. The player can take up one or several. Completing one alters the game state in specific, permanent ways. No mission is required to finish the main arc — except Thavian's, which is required for Ending ζ — but each mission completed deepens the final credits and opens dialogue elsewhere in the Order.

### Ioran Kest's Last Field Investigation (43 years cold)

**What he was doing:** Tracking the early movements of what he would later identify as the Fourth faction. His final field entry, found in his dossier in the Tessarene archive, names a Perfected Coast contact he was about to meet — a mid-ranking biologist in the pre-Liana Arvaux program. Ioran suspected the Fourth had their hands inside the Arvaux breeding work from the beginning.

**What completing the mission looks like:** The player finds the biologist, now elderly, on a quiet Coast retirement world. The old man remembers Ioran. He tells the player what he told Ioran, which Ioran never got to record. The Fourth faction guided the Arvaux program for three generations specifically to cultivate a "clean" bloodline that could replace the Vesh — a second act of Korien Vael's original founding move, pre-positioned for when the Vesh line failed.

**Consequences:** Liana Arvaux is revealed as something she herself does not know — a candidate the Fourth has been grooming toward the throne without her awareness. The reveal breaks open her entire arc. The player can tell her. She will not believe them at first. The evidence is Ioran's handwritten field notes, which the biologist has kept for 43 years, still in Ioran's handwriting.

**Unlocked endings:** Adds a Liana variant to Ending β that is significantly darker and more specific.

### Adhvi Ren's Murder Investigation (11 months cold)

**What she was doing:** Investigating Auriel Vesh's death. She had reached, via the Keepers, the finding that Imrys was involved. She was writing her confession entry the night she died. The last twelve sentences survive. They are torn.

**What completing the mission looks like:** The player finds the torn pages. Some are in Adhvi's cabin effects on *Impartial Regard* itself — sealed in the safe the current Witness inherited without knowing the combination. Some are in the Reliquary, in a place Adhvi hid them before she was killed. One is in Imrys's private chapel. Retrieving the last one requires confronting Imrys directly. He will confess. He will not fight. He asks only to be allowed to die at the foot of the altar where he laid Auriel's heart.

**Consequences:** Imrys can be killed by the Witness, or handed to Sevasti (Orthodox punishment), or allowed to live and be consumed by his own theology (he dies within a year regardless, of shame). Each option writes differently in the Record. Handing him to Sevasti specifically opens a dialogue where Sevasti explains, for the first time, why she poisoned Auriel — the full conversation is gated behind this handover.

**Unlocked endings:** Required for Ending α (Name Elara) to produce its full epilogue. Without it, the Elara ending still occurs but feels incomplete — the player never learns the truth of Auriel's death, and the credits omit the intercut letters.

### Thavian Ost's Fourth Forgery (4 centuries cold)

**What he was doing:** Thavian forged three Concord plate signatures during the Mad Years, preventing civil war. He was preparing to forge a fourth — the Serev bloodline's signature — when he died. The Serev bloodline was in succession crisis, and Thavian judged that forging the signature would stabilize the galaxy for a generation. His materials survive in the Tessarene vault — specifically, his wax mold of the Serev seal, his prepared ink, and his draft of the signature itself in his own hand.

**What completing the mission looks like:** The player retrieves the materials. They travel to the Argent Court. They use Thavian's mold to press the Serev signature onto the current Concord plate — which still bears an empty slot where Andrei Serev's living signature should have been renewed. The act is a forgery. It is also a completion of nine-century-old institutional work. The plate accepts the signature.

**Consequences:** The Hollow claim becomes, procedurally, a valid claim. Sevasti's three-year forgery of Serev edicts becomes retroactively justified — a signed Serev seal legally authorizes everything she has done. All nine bloodlines must now come to the table to formalize any new imperial claim, because all nine seats are represented. The resulting summit is the setting for Ending ζ.

**Unlocked endings:** Required for Ending ζ (The Forged Ninth Signature).

### Veyla Mir's Unsent Letter (2 centuries cold)

**What she was doing:** Veyla strangled Empress Kaeline Vesh — Auriel's great-grandmother — the night after Kaeline had publicly committed to disclosing the Long Record. Veyla's confession ends: *"I wrote her a letter the night before. I never sent it. I keep it still."*

**What completing the mission looks like:** The letter is in Veyla's personal effects, in a locked drawer of a minor cabinet in the Tessarene bindery. The drawer's key was swallowed by Veyla on her deathbed and buried with her. The player can retrieve it — a quiet, grim, short quest in the Order's own graveyard. The letter, read, is three pages long. Veyla agreed with Kaeline. Veyla killed her anyway. The letter explains why, and the reason is honest and terrible and exactly the reason Witnesses have always given: *"Someone must maintain the lie or everyone must die."*

**Consequences:** Reading the letter unlocks a dialogue option with Sevasti available nowhere else — a conversation about obedience and whether it has a shape. This dialogue is the only way to earn Sevasti's full trust without sheltering Kestrel, which means it is the only Orthodox-Witness path to Ending ε (Take the Throne).

**Unlocked endings:** Alternative path to Ending ε for Orthodox-faction Witnesses.

### Korien Vael's Empty Volume (9 centuries cold)

**What she was doing:** Korien Vael wrote her confession across eleven volumes. The twelfth, which she had bound and placed on the shelf beside the others, she left blank. Her final act before her death was to place it on the shelf and leave it empty.

**What completing the mission looks like:** This is not a quest in the conventional sense. It is an act. The volume is in the Tessarene vault beneath a pane of preserved glass. The player can, under specific late-game conditions, request the pane be raised. The bindery sisters raise it. The player opens the volume. The volume is blank. The player writes the first entry.

**Consequences:** Writing in Korien's empty volume is the act of committing Ending ζ. It is the foundation of the new Long Record that the Order will keep under its new, honest name. It is also the moment the Witness's current character fully accepts what they are doing: continuing Korien's work openly, nine centuries late.

**Unlocked endings:** Committed in Ending ζ. The player's chosen opening phrasing appears in the credits.

## XV. The Sixth Ending

### Ending ζ — **The Forged Ninth Signature.**

*Requirements:* must have completed Thavian Ost's Fourth Forgery. Must have earned Sevasti's full trust (through Adhvi Ren's investigation, through sheltering Kestrel, or through reading Veyla Mir's unsent letter). Must have refused to name any claimant by the summit. Must have opened Korien Vael's empty volume. Must be alive at the Argent Court summit.

*The plan:* The Witness forges the Serev signature using Thavian's nine-century-old mold. They convene an Argent Court summit with all nine bloodlines under Concord protocol. Their intent is to place the Long Record on the table as collateral and negotiate a constitutional compromise that ends the Touched killings and frees Elara. The plan is careful, bureaucratic, and nearly clever enough.

*The trap:* The Fourth faction has been waiting nine centuries for exactly this move. An Order Witness publicly attempting institutional repair is the one category of adversary Korien Vael anticipated when she designed the Order's internal architecture. The Fourth has dossiers on every living Witness. They have known for months that the player has been assembling Thavian's forgery materials. They let it happen.

On the second day of testimony, Brother Vassen Kir — Orthodox elder, sleeper Fourth — rises and presents Thavian Ost's original draft, preserved in the Tessarene vault in a drawer the player did not know existed. He presents it alongside chain-of-custody evidence placing the player at the vault on the day the mold was removed. The nine bloodlines are shown, with full Order authority, that the Serev signature on their summit plate is a forgery committed by the Witness before them.

The summit collapses within the hour. The bloodlines scatter under armed escort. The Concord protocol that protected the session now protects nothing — the session has been invalidated from within. The Fourth, moving faster than the shock allows any other faction to react, seizes control of Tessarene by nightfall. The motherhouse doors are closed to the Orthodox and the Prescient. Sevasti is arrested. Kestrel, if aboard the ship, is arrested. The Long Record's master volumes are placed under Fourth seal.

*The offer:* Vassen Kir comes to the Witness personally, within 48 hours, at a location the player does not choose — usually the Tessarene bindery, with the two elderly sisters present and silent. The offer is specific. The Fourth will clear the Witness's name publicly. They will attribute the forged signature to Sevasti, who will be executed for it whether the Witness cooperates or not. They will restore the Order. They will stabilize the Concord under a reformed covenant that makes the Touched killings a matter of public law rather than private crime — harder to hide, easier to justify, and no longer requiring individual Witnesses to bear them alone.

The Witness's cooperation requires one act: kill Elara. Personally. Not as punishment. As founding.

Vassen Kir explains, quietly, that this is what Korien did. Korien's founding act was a murder of a child. Every Order renewal since has been delayed by the Order's refusal to repeat the act. The Fourth believes the Order is due another founding. The Witness, by forging the signature and forcing the summit and bringing the Order's secrets to the light, has created the conditions under which the new founding is possible. The player did not mean to do this. But they did it.

Refusing the offer is Ending γ-variant. The Fourth hands the Witness to whichever bloodline will pay most — usually Kroft, who wants a Witness subject for his laboratory. The player dies in the Iron Parochy within two weeks. The galaxy, leaderless and with the Order publicly compromised, goes to full war within the month. Elara is killed in the Nursery when the regents' protection fails on the fourteenth night after the summit's collapse. Credits over footage of the Nursery's library on fire. The hollow wall is unopened. Auriel's letters burn unread.

*The act:* Accepting is the real Ending ζ. The player travels to the Nursery alone. Elara knows. She has known since nine. She is waiting for the Witness in the library, having removed the regents to another wing under a pretext of her own devising. She has laid out a small meal — bread, a cup of warm wine, three apples — in the room where her mother died in childbirth. She asks the Witness to eat with her first. The player can refuse. The player can eat. Either is allowed to commit the act.

Elara does not fight. She does not plead. She hands the Witness the knife from Auriel's letters — a small Order blade Auriel had left her for this specific purpose, in case no better path opened. She tells the Witness: *"I asked him to choose for me. He gave me the choice. I give it to you. Do it well."* The player holds the knife.

The act is committed in first person, at a camera angle of the player's choosing. The game offers four camera framings, each of which is the player's small mercy or small cruelty. The game saves immediately after.

*The consequence:* The Witness returns to Tessarene. The Fourth restores the Order. The player's name is cleared. Sevasti is executed on a gallows in the motherhouse courtyard. The player watches. Kestrel, if aboard, is executed three days later in a separate ceremony. Meridian, if aboard, leaves the ship and is never heard from again. Vaspar, if aboard, stays. He tells the player: *"I thought it would be worse. Is that not worse?"*

The Touched killings become a matter of public law under the Reformed Covenant. The hidden asylums are emptied — the Visible and Hybrid Vesh are executed systematically over the next eighteen months. The player is present at three of these, as senior Witness. The player does not kill any of them personally. They bear witness to the killings. That is the role of the Order under the Reformed Covenant. Every Witness bears public witness now. It is cleaner than the old way, Vassen says. It is cleaner.

The player is made Elder of the Reformed Order at year three. They live another forty years. Over those forty years, they personally kill seven more Touched children — all quiet, all private exceptions, all cases where a bloodline family begged the Order to act without public witness. Each killing is logged in Korien's volume, which is no longer empty. The first entry is the player's own, the night after Elara. The player's chosen phrasing. Four options:

- *"She gave me the knife. I used it well. I will not write about it again."*
- *"I am what I have always been. I am what the Order is. I am what Korien was. I have crossed the Delaware."*
- *"I think she forgave me. I do not know. I will not forgive myself."*
- *[Leave the page blank and close the book.]*

The fourth option is selectable. It commits an empty page. The player's successor, when they read it, will find a single pressed petal inside — from the apples Elara served. The game remembers the petal. No other entry in the Long Record contains a pressed flower.

*Epilogue:* The player at seventy. At Tessarene. Placing the eighth child's body in the vault. Winter. Snow against the bindery windows. The two elderly sisters watch from the shelves, as they have watched since the player was young. One of them was there the day the player first wrote in Korien's empty volume, the day after Elara. She remembers who the player used to be. She does not speak. She has not spoken in thirty years.

The player closes the vault. Walks up the stairs. Opens the Long Record at the current volume and writes the child's entry. The handwriting is steady. The entry is three sentences. The game does not show the player what the sentences say. The credits roll over the sound of the pen.

The last image is Korien Vael's first volume, on the shelf behind the player, open to her first page. Her handwriting and the player's handwriting are, side by side, indistinguishable.

*This is not the anti-hero ending. This is the ending where the game's villain wins by using the player's best instincts against them. Every clever choice the player made to reach this ending was a choice Korien Vael anticipated. The forgery kit was left accessible for exactly this purpose. The empty volume was left empty so someone exactly like the player would one day fill it. The Fourth did not corrupt the player. The Order did. By design. Nine centuries ago.*

*There is no path from this ending to any of the other five. There is no "rescue" version. The player cannot reload an earlier save — the Long Record does not permit it, the act is committed, the save was written before the killing and again after. The player chose this by choosing every step that led here. That is the point.*

## XVI. Visual Language (v3)

Broad notes preserved from v2. Additions:

**Bloodline imagery.** Touched children and Hybrid cousins should be rendered with restraint, not spectacle. A specific visual language: features that are *just* wrong enough to be beautiful and disturbing at the same time. An extra row of teeth. Eyes set slightly too wide. A limb that hangs in a way human limbs do not. Never body horror for its own sake — always the suggestion that the bearer was loved by someone. Visible Vesh in the hidden asylums should be presented with the dignity of religious paintings of saints. They should be the most moving images in the game.

**Tessarene vault.** Deep underground. Stone. Candles. The Long Record fills the walls — bound volumes stacked floor to ceiling, nine centuries deep. The vault smells, the text should say, of old vellum and incense. Korien Vael's original confession is in the center, in a glass case, in her own handwriting, in a language the player cannot read without finding a specific item first. The player may or may not ever read it translated. Most players will not.

**Gift visualization.** When a Gifted character uses their Gift in-game — Elara tasting blood, Damar feeling pain, Aldric waking — the screen treatment shifts specifically. Not a flashy effect. A slight desaturation. A subtle warp of silhouettes against backgrounds. A feeling that the world has briefly remembered it is not quite real.

**The Witness silhouette.** Grey robes. Belted. Hooded when formal. The Order signet — three concentric rings, and within the innermost, a mark no one outside the Order can read. The mark, if the player looks closely, is a stylized single word in the Order's private script: *Korien.* Every Witness wears her name without knowing it. One of the late-game reveals, for a particular dialogue branch with Sister Olen Rho, is this fact.

## XVII. Gameplay Implications

The bloodline-and-Order framework means:

- **Space travel is safer than v2.** The dimming is not a malevolent advance. It is infrastructure failure. Some jump routes go dark over the course of a playthrough. This is visible on the star map. The player can mitigate it by completing specific Vesh-technology questlines. The pace of dimming is slower than v2 and feels more like mourning than dread.
- **Planet dungeons are deeply authored.** Each one tells the story of a claimant *and* the story of the previous Witness or Witnesses who worked that cluster. Environmental storytelling does heavy lifting. Old Order graffiti. Marks on walls. Hidden vaults.
- **Companions are central.** Ship cabin time becomes a meaningful part of the game. Dialogue. Reading. Letters. Slow friendship. Slow grief.
- **Combat in dungeons is Souls-weight.** Bosses are either a claimant, a claimant's champion, a Touched or Hybrid cousin in extremis, or — in the secret endings — another Witness. The fight against Brother Vassen Kir, if it happens, is a duel, not a brawl.
- **Ship combat exists but is avoidable.** Pirates, Kroft's agents hunting the ship, Keeper interception attempts. The ship has the ceremonial chaff-launcher and the player can choose to flee or evade. Fights won feel like triumphs. Fights lost do not end the run but lose cargo and damage the ship.
- **Moral choices are structural, not binary.** A quest does not have a "good" and "evil" button. It has a set of actors with incompatible legitimate claims, and the Witness decides who to side with, based on information that is always incomplete. The game judges the player by what they chose knowing what they knew. The dossier records this. The next Witness reads it.
