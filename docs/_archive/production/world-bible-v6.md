# BLACK STAR — World Bible v6

*Codename: The Ninth Heir*
*Snapshot: 2026-04-21 (late evening)*
*Supersedes v5. This is the authoritative canon for story, design, art direction, and workflow.*

---

## Writer's Intent

This is an anti-hero epic. The player is not a hero. The player is the latest in a nine-century chain of Witnesses who have quietly shaped the galaxy from behind a mask of neutrality, each doing what they believed was right, each making the next Witness's world worse. The Order is the villain. The bloodline it has preserved is the villain. The player, inheriting both, is the villain.

The story is told in the tradition of **GRRM** (political texture, no pure sides), **Tolkien** (deep time, names of things long gone), **Lovecraft** (knowledge as contamination), **Dune** (bloodline as weapon, prescience as curse), **Attack on Titan** (hero-becomes-monster, generational inherited sin), and **Gurren Lagann** (the one who crosses the Delaware — spirals of defiance, even unto ruin).

At the end of a full playthrough, the credits should land like a weight on the chest. The player has won nothing clean. They have chosen what to be complicit in. The second run is where the story opens.

---

## Hard Rules (do not violate)

- No "power of friendship" endings. Every outcome is complicity.
- Heaviness throughout, not saved for endings.
- Ending ζ has no escape hatch. The player chose it.
- The Witness is small, quiet, documentary. Not a heroic warrior.
- The ship is a pen, not a sword.
- One Witness alive at a time. Always.
- The "dimming" is biological/infrastructural failure, NOT cosmic horror. The Penultima was cut in v3 revision; it survives only as a myth Auriel Vesh invented to distract the Keepers.
- The cosmic threat is the Vesh Gift itself, destabilizing across nine centuries of forced maintenance.

# PART ONE — STORY CANON

## I. The Setting — The Concord, the Vesh, and the Order

The galaxy has been unified for seven centuries under the **House of Vesh**, governed through nine covenants called the **Concord** chained as steel plates to an orbital throne-ship, the **Argent Court**, near the galactic core.

Eleven years ago, Emperor **Auriel Vesh** died. He had no heirs he acknowledged. The reaffirmation went unsigned. The nine great bloodlines that signed the original Concord now each claim the throne.

The galaxy has not yet gone to war. Everyone is waiting to see who blinks.

The **Witness Order** was founded nine centuries ago to maintain the plates and adjudicate disputes. Witnesses have no formal political power. Their power is epistemic — when a Witness declares a fact about the Concord, no one has successfully contested it.

This is the story every child in the galaxy learns. It is a lie of omission. The Order has never been neutral.

## II. The Pre-Vesh Era — The Thelenor Fall

Before the Vesh, the imperial line was the **Thelenor** — a dynasty older than recorded history, who ruled for eleven centuries because "they had always ruled." The Thelenor fell in a single year: a coordinated plague — or poisoning, no record is trustworthy — took the whole bloodline. Thirty-seven people in fourteen months. The last Thelenor, an eleven-year-old girl named **Miriath**, died in the Argent Court itself. Her body was never recovered.

The First Witness, **Sister Korien Vael**, was the woman who found Miriath dead. She was also, the player will eventually learn, the one who poisoned her.

Korien's reason: the Thelenor were *touched*. Their bloodline bore something — a mutation, a gift, a curse; the language is inadequate — that had grown across eleven centuries into something the galaxy could not survive. The last Thelenor generation was producing children who were not children. Miriath herself was still human. Her younger cousin was not. Korien killed Miriath to end the line before the non-human cousins could inherit.

The four years of chaos that followed, the years in which **Orin Vesh** rose, were not politics. They were Korien Vael manufacturing a new dynasty. She chose the Vesh — minor coreward nobility, clean blood, no taint — and she *made* them imperial. She assembled the nine bloodlines that would become the Concord signatories. She drafted the plates. She invented the ceremonial role of the Witness Order to monitor what she had just done, and then she swore her successors to forget.

The plates themselves contain her name nowhere. She destroyed every record of her involvement. The Order remembers her as "the First Witness," mother of the oath, moral exemplar. The truth is in a vault beneath the Order's motherhouse on the planet **Tessarene**. The player's Witness will access that vault in the last act.

## III. The Vesh Bloodline — The Gift

Korien Vael did not save the galaxy. She traded it.

The Vesh line she elevated was clean, yes. But Korien was not just removing a dangerous gift from the imperial chair — she was putting a different one in its place. Orin Vesh had a cousin who could see the near-future. A sister who never aged. An uncle who could speak to the dead. The Vesh line, Korien decided, was a safer vessel for imperial-scale prescience than the Thelenor had become. Safer. Not clean.

For seven centuries, the Vesh line has been haunted by its gift. Most are born normal. A significant minority are not.

**The Visible.** Born with visible anomaly. Extra vertebrae. Mirrored organs. Limbs that end wrong. Skin that responds to light. Historically these children have been killed at birth, quietly, with records falsified. Current living count of Visible Vesh in hidden asylums across the galaxy: four. The player can find them. They do not forgive what was done to them.

**The Hybrid.** Born bearing traits of other species, other taxonomic kingdoms. A Vesh cousin in the 6th century who grew antlers. A Vesh duchess in the 9th who breathed underwater. Current generation: Ser Nadezh Vol is one — her trait is sleeplessness, which she has not disclosed publicly. She has been awake for fifty-one years.

**The Gifted.** Born normal in appearance. Touched in other ways. Prescience. Memory perfection. The ability to charm, to persuade beyond natural limits. The ability to speak languages they have never learned. Auriel Vesh was Gifted. Elara is Gifted. Most of the nine claimants have Gifted ancestors in the last three generations.

**The Touched.** Born with a specific and dangerous pattern that shows up once every three or four generations — a child that is, in Korien's old private language, "Thelenor-shaped." An ancestor's face. An ancestor's gifts. Sometimes an ancestor's goals. The Touched, when they appear, are killed. This has always been Witness Order work. Witnesses have murdered Vesh children for seven centuries to maintain the line's stability. Every Witness who has killed a Touched child has the record in their dossier.

Elara Vesh is Gifted. The child in Liana Arvaux's bio-lab — the ninth heir of the Perfected Coast — is Touched. This is why Liana is trying to breed her line perfectly: she has a Touched daughter she cannot bear to kill, and she is trying to out-breed the shape.

The bloodline is coming apart. The last three generations have produced unusually many Gifted and Touched. The Order's private projection: the next generation — Elara's children, if she has them — will be mostly Hybrid or Touched. The line will collapse into the thing Korien killed Miriath to prevent.

**This is the cosmic threat. Not a god. Not the Penultima. It is in the blood. It has been in the blood for nine centuries. The galaxy has been kept stable at the cost of a specific ongoing crime that the Witness Order has been committing for all nine of those centuries.**

The stars are *not* dimming supernaturally. What the Verge people are calling the "dimming" is actually the visible failure of the Concord's quiet mechanisms — Vesh-gift-powered logistics, Vesh-gift-powered jump-route stability, Vesh-gift-powered communications. Auriel's death didn't just leave a political vacuum. It withdrew a technological substrate the galaxy had forgotten was being provided. The Verge is going dark because the distributed prescient network the Vesh line has quietly maintained for seven centuries is starving without its central node.

Elara, trained correctly and willing, could restore it. But she is eleven. And the training is not painless. And the Order intends to put her through it anyway.

## IV. The Emperor Auriel Vesh

Auriel was the forty-seventh Vesh Emperor. He reigned forty-one years. He was Gifted — specifically, he could see the approximate shapes of the near future, in fragments, in dreams. He married late and had no legitimate children. The reason: he had seen his own children and could not bear to sire them.

In his last decade, Auriel did something no Vesh Emperor had done: he tried to end his own line.

He stopped attending court. He sealed his library. He funded Verge-edge expeditions that pretended to look for the **Penultima** (a myth he invented to distract the Keepers) while actually looking for every hidden Visible and Touched and Gifted Vesh cousin in the galaxy. He wrote to each of them. He offered them one of three things: quiet extraction to the Verge, honest conversation, or a clean death of their choosing.

He corresponded with three people at the end: **Mother Sevasti**, **Ser Nadezh Vol**, and **Elara Vesh**. He asked Sevasti to open the Order's oldest vault — the one under Tessarene — and read the truth of Korien Vael. She did. She has not yet told anyone what she read.

He asked Nadezh to take the throne briefly, sign one law that would dissolve the Concord, and then die cleanly. She refused. Not from cowardice. She told him: *this has to be done by someone who does not know what they are doing. It is the last gift you can give the galaxy.*

He asked Elara nothing. He only wrote to her. He told her everything he knew. He left her to decide.

Auriel was murdered. The physician of record, Elder Brother Imrys of the Keepers, was bribed to sign the "organ failure" finding. Auriel was poisoned by a Witness on Sevasti's order. Sevasti did it to fulfill a request Auriel had made of her personally: *when I cannot do it myself, you will do it. Do not let my Gift outlive my will.* Sevasti did her duty. It has broken her. She has spent eleven years trying to make it worth what she did.

## V. The Witness Order

The Order's public history: neutral observers, keepers of the Concord, loyal only to truth.

The Order's real history: a covert political apparatus that has murdered Vesh children for nine centuries, assassinated heads of state an average of once per century, forged documents at every level up to and including the Concord plates themselves, and engineered at least four "natural" disasters to collapse states that threatened the stability of the line it was secretly preserving.

All of this is in the Order's private archives, organized by Witness. Each Witness who joins takes a second oath — never spoken aloud — to the **Long Record**. Witnesses read their predecessors' confessions. Witnesses write their own. A Witness does not die without her final testimony in the Long Record.

### Reframe (2026-04-21)

The Order is not cartoonishly evil. They are arithmeticians with a theology. For 900 years they have done the math: the galaxy collapses without the Vesh line; preserving the line is holy; what preserves the line is permissible.

**They do not orchestrate tragedy. They wait for it.**

They watch battlefields, plague wards, condemned cells, ruined houses. When you fall, they are there with surgery, with coin, with a signet, with mercy. You are not a victim of the Order. You are a victim of the world whom the Order happened to save. You will owe them for it. You will not be allowed to forget.

### The Witness as Singular Apex

The Witness is the Order's apex position, and only one Witness is alive at a time.
- The Order has exactly one Witness at any time.
- When a Witness dies, the Order has no Witness until the replacement is made via the Body-Bell-Binding ritual (see Part Two).
- The player's Witness is unique in the galaxy while alive — not one among many, *the* Witness.
- Sevasti's letters, claimant receptions, and Order internal operations all assume a single Witness.
- Ending ζ's exposure-of-the-forgery carries additional weight: while the Order purges and replaces the disgraced Witness, the Order is *without* a Witness, which has no historical precedent.

### Order Hierarchy (partial)

The Order has full internal class structure below Witness. Not yet fully designed. What is known:

- **Witness** — apex. One alive. Judicial, documentary, travels freely.
- **Takers** — non-Witness Order enforcers who retrieve dead Witnesses' signets, perform Takings, escort claimant delegations, execute Order discipline at a low level. They appear in Act 0 and Act III of the opening, bookending the ritual. Witnesses outrank them.
- **Inquisitors** (tentative) — investigators of Witness misconduct. Trained with Witnesses; know their techniques. May have personally known prior Witnesses.
- **Elders** — faction leaders, archive keepers, Tessarene bindery sisters, senior administrators.
- **Scribes** — maintain the Long Record's master volumes. The two elderly Tessarene bindery sisters are of this class.

Full hierarchy is a dedicated future session.

### The Four Factions

**The Orthodox.** Believe the Long Record is necessary. The oath is intact. Led by **Elder Brother Vassen Kir**, a 78-year-old with beautiful manners who has personally killed thirteen Touched children in his career. (Sleeper Fourth — revealed in Ending ζ.)

**The Prescient.** Led by **Mother Sevasti**. Believe the Order must prevent the collapse it sees coming, even if that means working against the Concord directly. Sevasti has been forging edicts under the dead claimant Andrei Serev's name for three years.

**The Dissolved.** Small, heretical. Believe the Order itself is the ongoing crime and must end. Led, if led is the word, by **Sister Olen Rho**, a former Prescient who crossed over when she could no longer bear the killings.

**The Fourth.** Not acknowledged. Not officially real. Loyal to Korien Vael personally — to her original logic, to her willingness to do the unforgivable for the sake of continuity. They believe Elara should be killed now, before she is trained. They have attempted this once already in the game's backstory.

The player's Witness's faction is randomized within the character-creation disposition system. Factions are NOT named on the creation screen. The Order assigns based on answers + seeded RNG. The player discovers their faction through NPC reactions in-game.

### Internal Moral Composition (orthogonal to faction)

Within the Order's ranks, moral axes cut across factions:
- **The Reformers** — genuine believers who have spent decades trying to change the Order from within. Old, tired, mostly unsuccessful.
- **The Butchers** — those who enjoy the cruelty. The robes are cover.
- **The Merchants** — selling signet authority, indulging corruption for profit.
- **The Liturgists** — true believers in the math. The Order's spine. Neither cruel nor kind. Exhausted. **Sevasti is a Liturgist.**
- **The Hollow** — Witnesses who have stopped believing anything but cannot leave.

A Butcher Orthodox and a Butcher Dissolved are different kinds of dangerous.

## VI. Previous Witnesses

The Order's Long Record is in-game content. Fragments are readable in the motherhouse library or in *Impartial Regard*'s cabin safe.

**Sister Korien Vael (9 centuries ago).** Poisoner of Miriath Thelenor. Founder of the Order. The original crime. Left an empty twelfth volume — writing in it is the act of committing Ending ζ.

**Brother Thavian Ost (4 centuries ago).** Forged three Concord plate signatures during the Mad Years when two claimants went to war over a dead emperor. The forgeries held. The war ended. His Long Record confession runs 40 pages. The plates still bear his forgery. Nobody else knows. His mold is required for Ending ζ.

**Sister Veyla Mir (2 centuries ago).** Assassinated a reigning Vesh empress — Auriel's great-grandmother — who had decided to publicly disclose the Long Record. Strangled her in her bath. Her confession is one paragraph. The empress was, by Veyla's own account, correct to want disclosure.

**Brother Ioran Kest (43 years ago).** Disappeared mid-mission. No confession. Order considers his file permanently open. In fact, Ioran defected to the Fourth faction, then horrified by what he learned about himself, walked away from everything. He is alive on a Verge moon. He is the reason Nadezh renounced her claim and the reason Damar Hul carved his skin. He has a hidden questline.

**Sister Adhvi Ren (the player's direct predecessor).** Murdered. Incomplete confession. Believed the Order was preparing to kill Elara. Pages end mid-sentence. Plot thread through the whole game. Killed by an Order member. The player will suspect the wrong person first.

## VII. The Nine Claimants

Each is a planet cluster. Each a dungeon. Each morally complex.

1. **Damar Hul** — The Penitent King, Thorned Provinces. Carves prayer-sigils into his skin. Gifted (pain empathy). Committed genocide in the Crown Wars. Ioran Kest witnessed and did not report.

2. **Liana Arvaux** — Perfected Coast, bio-lab. Breeding her line perfectly because she has a Touched daughter she cannot bear to kill. Ninth heir is Touched.

3. **Aldric, the Dreaming Patriarch** — can be woken, which destroys his grandchildren who have ruled well.

4. **The Glass Empress** — ninth heir is six years old.

5. **Kroft** — cybernetic, dangerous. Wants a Witness for his laboratory. Ending-γ-variant hands the player to Kroft.

6. **Elara Vesh** — See Section X.

7. **Ser Nadezh Vol** — The Exile, The Verge. Auriel's great hope. Hybrid (sleepless, fifty-one years awake). Runs an orphanage hiding Verge-born Vesh descendants. Will take the throne only if the Witness asks. Thinks there is a 40% chance she becomes Touched under throne pressure.

8. **The Sevened** — New Concord, Senate Stars. Counselor **Ise Varn** has reasoned out 80% of the bloodline truth. Offers constitutional stability purchased with Elara's specific death.

9. **The Hollow — Name of Serev, Orphan Worlds.** The claim is a forgery. Mother Sevasti forges edicts under Andrei Serev's name. The Orphan Worlds know, and have colluded in the forgery for three years.

## VIII. Elara Vesh

Eleven at game start. Fourteen or fifteen by game end, if she lives.

Gifted. Specifically: *resonant* Gifted — she can briefly borrow the gift of anyone whose blood she has tasted. She has knowingly tasted three people's blood: her mother (died in childbirth, visionary dreams for a week), her regent Elder Brother Imrys (bandaged her cut hand when she was six — carries his emotional detachment), and Auriel (he cut his own palm for her at nine — she has his prescience in fragments).

Auriel wrote to her for two years. Letters hidden in a hollow wall of the Nursery library. She has read them all. She has known since nine that she will almost certainly die before twenty. She has chosen how she wants it to happen: with dignity, on her terms, after meeting the Witness Auriel promised her.

She is not a victim. She lies. She tells the truth. She is capable of extraordinary affection. She can die many ways — poisoning, assassination, regent politics, an attempt to flee, the Fourth faction, a failed coronation, Ise Varn's clean execution. Most of those deaths can be prevented. Some require the Witness to break every oath. The game does not tell the Witness which is which.

She is the only character who speaks honestly about what the Vesh line is. She has visited the hidden asylum on Mentiel and met her Visible cousins. She will, if the Witness earns her full trust, ask one specific thing: *when I die, whatever way I die, find them, and do not let the Order finish them.*

The hidden asylums are the endgame's quietest thread. The Order will, if the Vesh line ends, execute the remaining Visible and Hybrid Vesh to close the books. The Witness can prevent this. Most endings do not prevent this. One does.

### Canonized Distillation

*"She has read the letters. She has done the math. She does not fear death. She fears what her death will cost others."*

## IX. Mother Sevasti

The Witness's Order superior. Prescient faction. Liturgist temperament. Poisoned Auriel at his own request. Has forged dead-claimant edicts for three years to buy time. Reads the truth of Korien Vael in the Tessarene vault at Auriel's request. Has not told anyone.

She is the closest thing the Witness has to an ally within the Order. She has broken her oath for three years. She will ask the Witness to join her in breaking it further.

After the Tessarene vault revelation in Mid-Game Turn 2, she will offer to resign — to step aside and let the Witness decide what the Order should be. She means it. She has been waiting eleven years for someone to reach that point.

## X. Companions

Three. None fight. Each has a betrayal-adjacent arc revealed in Mid-Game Turn 3.

**Meridian.** Veteran of the Crown Wars. Deserted after Damar's atrocity. On the ship because Sevasti sent her. She knows Crown Wars history in ways Order records do not. Will eventually discover Ioran Kest was present at Damar's crime and did not stop it. If the player pursues Ioran's questline, Meridian demands confrontation. Three outcomes, none clean.

**Vaspar.** Perfected Coast defector. Early 30s. Soft-spoken. Was being groomed as Liana Arvaux's ninth heir's tutor before he escaped. Knows the bio-lab from inside. He is the sixth heir of the program — an earlier attempt, judged inadequate, released. He thinks of himself as flawed. He is extraordinary. If the player learns this and tells him, he breaks. If the player does not tell him, he helps sabotage Liana's program and leaves mid-game believing he was disposable.

**Kestrel.** Dissolved Witness. Younger than the player. Angry. Defected because she was asked to kill a Touched infant and refused. Came aboard *Impartial Regard* as a stowaway in the first act. Player can turn her in (clean, Kestrel executed) or shelter her (reliable ally, opens Olen Rho's questline, but if Sevasti ever discovers her the run ends badly).

## XI. The Three Mid-Game Turns

**First Turn — The Dossier (~30% through).** Witness completes own Long Record entries. Discovers Sister Adhvi Ren's unfinished confession. Pages end mid-sentence. Witness now knows Adhvi was murdered and suspects the killer was in the Order. Will suspect wrong person first. Brother Vassen Kir encourages dropping the investigation.

**Second Turn — The Vault (~60% through).** Witness accesses Tessarene vault. Reads truth of Korien Vael. No cutscene. Player sits and reads. After reading, the Order's entire game changes. Sevasti offers to resign.

**Third Turn — The Companion (~70-85% through).** The closest companion is revealed as deliberately placed. By Sevasti (Meridian), Liana (Vaspar), or the Fourth (Kestrel). Each revelation has a 30-second dialogue window.

## XII. The Six Endings

All are complicity. None resolve cleanly.

### Ending α — Name Elara
She refuses the throne publicly in the Concord's ritual language. The line is broken. The Gift dissipates across a generation. Millions die in transitional collapses. No more Touched children are born. Elara lives. She is fifteen, goes to the Verge with Nadezh, lives to old age. Order fractures. Long Record burned voluntarily.
*Epilogue:* Elara at forty on a Verge beach. Two silhouettes. No dialogue.

### Ending β — Name Nadezh
She accepts because the Witness asked. Reigns 23 years. Best emperor since the Thelenor fall. Near the end becomes Touched under throne pressure. Dies. Order continues killing Touched children. 23 years of stability for the cost of Nadezh's mind.
*Epilogue:* Nadezh at the end, dictating a letter to a commoner child she raised.

### Ending γ — Dissolve the Concord with the Sevened
Ise Varn takes the Argent Court. Elara gives public testimony. Elara murdered in her bed three weeks later by a mourning noble. Galaxy fragments into constitutional experiments. Two centuries later, Vesh Gift rediscovered in a sequestered population. The cycle begins again.
*Epilogue:* Montage across two centuries. A Gifted child four centuries from now. A new Witness Order born again without knowing its history.

### Ending δ — Burn the Record
Witness destroys the Long Record personally in Tessarene vault. Order loses institutional memory within a generation. Elara's fate depends on what the Witness told her first.
*Epilogue:* Witness alone in the ashes, reciting Korien's confession from memory.

### Ending ε — Take the Throne (hidden, high requirements)
Requirements: defected faction at least once, sheltered Kestrel, read the vault, met Ioran, refused Nadezh's offer, extracted Elara to the Verge, holds Sevasti's Concord access tokens.
The Witness goes to the Argent Court alone. The plates refuse. The Witness signs with the Order's signet and the Long Record itself. The plates accept the Order as the bloodline. The Order *is* the bloodline. The Gift leaves. Elara lives, furious. The Witness rules 40 years as the first explicitly acknowledged Order Emperor.
*Epilogue:* The Witness at 70 on the throne, dictating the new Long Record's first entry: *"I was the one who crossed the Delaware."*

### Ending ζ — The Forged Ninth Signature (no escape hatch)
Requirements: Thavian Ost's Fourth Forgery completed, Sevasti's full trust, refused to name any claimant by the summit, opened Korien's empty volume, alive at the Argent Court summit.

The Witness forges the Serev signature using Thavian's nine-century-old mold. Convenes a summit. Places the Long Record on the table as collateral. Intends to negotiate an end to the Touched killings and free Elara.

The Fourth has been waiting nine centuries for exactly this move. They have known for months. On day two, Brother Vassen Kir (sleeper Fourth) presents Thavian's original draft and chain-of-custody evidence placing the Witness at the vault. The summit collapses. The Fourth seizes Tessarene. Sevasti is arrested. Kestrel, if aboard, is arrested. The Long Record master volumes are placed under Fourth seal.

Vassen Kir's offer: clear the Witness's name publicly (attributing the forgery to Sevasti, who will be executed either way), restore the Order, make Touched killings a matter of public law under a Reformed Covenant. Cost: the Witness kills Elara personally. Not as punishment. As founding.

Refusing is a variant of Ending γ. The Fourth hands the Witness to Kroft. The player dies in the Iron Parochy within two weeks. Elara is killed fourteen nights later. The Nursery library burns. Auriel's letters burn unread.

Accepting is the real Ending ζ. The Witness travels to the Nursery alone. Elara knows. She is waiting in the library, having moved the regents away. A small meal — bread, warm wine, three apples — in the room where her mother died in childbirth. She asks the Witness to eat with her first. The player can refuse. The player can eat. Either is allowed.

Elara does not fight. She does not plead. She hands the Witness the knife from Auriel's letters — a small Order blade Auriel left her for this specific purpose. She tells the Witness: *"I asked him to choose for me. He gave me the choice. I give it to you. Do it well."* The player holds the knife.

The act is committed in first person, at a camera angle of the player's choosing. Four framings, each a small mercy or small cruelty. The game saves immediately after.

The Fourth restores the Order. The Witness's name is cleared. Sevasti is executed in the motherhouse courtyard; the Witness watches. The hidden asylums are emptied over eighteen months; the Witness is present at three executions, bears public witness, does not kill personally. That is the role of the Order under the Reformed Covenant.

The Witness is made Elder of the Reformed Order at year three. Lives another forty years. Personally kills seven more Touched children — all quiet, all private exceptions, all cases where a bloodline family begged the Order to act without public witness. Each killing logged in Korien's volume, which is no longer empty.

First entry is the player's own, the night after Elara. Four phrasings:
- *"She gave me the knife. I used it well. I will not write about it again."*
- *"I am what I have always been. I am what the Order is. I am what Korien was. I have crossed the Delaware."*
- *"I think she forgave me. I do not know. I will not forgive myself."*
- *[Leave the page blank and close the book.]*

The fourth commits an empty page. The player's successor, when they read it, finds a single pressed petal inside — from the apples Elara served. The game remembers the petal. No other entry in the Long Record contains a pressed flower.

*Epilogue:* The Witness at seventy. At Tessarene. Placing the eighth child's body in the vault. Winter. Snow against the bindery windows. The two elderly sisters watch from the shelves. One was there the day the player first wrote in Korien's empty volume. She has not spoken in thirty years.

The player closes the vault. Walks up the stairs. Opens the Long Record at the current volume and writes the child's entry. The handwriting is steady. The entry is three sentences. The game does not show what they say. Credits roll over the sound of the pen.

Last image: Korien Vael's first volume, on the shelf, open to her first page. Her handwriting and the player's handwriting, side by side, indistinguishable.

**No escape hatch. No reload-out. The Long Record does not permit it. The save was written before the killing and again after. The player chose this.**

## XIII. The Long Record

Not a menu. A rendered physical book: leather-bound, worn, half-full, sitting on a small desk in *Impartial Regard*'s cabin and reproduced at every Witness sanctum on every planet.

### Three Layers of Entries

**Your own entries.** After every significant event, the book opens and the Witness writes. Player chooses from 2-4 authored phrasings. Phrasings are never good/evil. They are tonal and specific.

**Predecessors' entries.** Fragments unlocked by in-world actions. Some always visible. Reading an unlocked entry may trigger a writing prompt — "What do you make of this?" — and the Witness must commit an opinion. Sevasti is watching. The successor will read your opinion of Veyla Mir before they read Veyla Mir.

**Unfinished missions.** Every previous Witness's dossier contains open field investigations interrupted by death, defection, despair. Five of six endings require at least one completed legacy mission. Ending ζ requires a specific one (Korien's empty volume).

### Commitment Model (Souls-style)

You write, it commits. No undo. The Long Record *is* the save system.

- **Resting on the ship** writes a brief status entry. Cheapest save.
- **Writing at a planetside Witness sanctum** saves the full game state. Sanctums are rare — one per cluster, sometimes two.
- **Death without a recent entry** means the successor inherits consequences without context.
- **Auto-save** exists only for crash recovery.

The player must learn to write *in advance of danger*. Before the boss. Before the confrontation.

### The Living Book

The book's physical state evolves: seals where the Witness judged, blots where predecessors shook, glued-in insertions, torn corners. Veyla Mir's confession has a specific blot near the word *"strangled."* Adhvi Ren's twelve surviving sentences end in a diagonal smear. Auriel's one-sentence forgery — *"She did not have the right"* — is slipped into Korien's founding volume. Sevasti placed it. The player finds it before understanding why.

# PART TWO — GAME DESIGN CANON

## Opening Sequence — Five Acts (Locked)

Shared with the Witness death/respawn loop. Every death reuses this ritual with the player's just-dead Witness in Act 0.

### Act 0 — The Body (prologue)

Black screen held silent. Top-down pixel composition of a dead Witness on a stone floor. Grey robes, signet at chest, short blade at left hip, closed Long Record beside. Rust-red thread visible at collar. Candle almost guttered. Body has been dead an hour.

Two **Takers** in the chamber. They do not speak. One kneels and removes the signet. The other lifts the closed Long Record.

First playthrough: body is a canonical predecessor. Death/respawn: body is the player's just-dead Witness.

### Act I — The Bell

One low bell strike. The Long Record opens on an intake desk. An invisible hand lifts the quill. The Order's founding rite writes itself in cramped ecclesiastical script (Korien Vael's original hand). Translation below in italics. Reads holy. Is cruel.

Rite completes. Page turns. The 900 names scroll past — nine centuries of Witnesses. Most recent name (the Witness just shown dead) in still-drying ink. Blank line beneath. Waiting.

Title card renders within the book: BLACK STAR / THE NINTH HEIR. Menu options render as marginalia: NEW WITNESS / CONTINUE / OPTIONS.

(Title screen implementation: see separate section below. This Act I title treatment is for when the player clicks through from the title screen into the ritual.)

### Act II — The Binding

Witness Creation. Silent except for quill and candle. Player builds:
- Appearance: skin tone, hair, face features, build, age bracket
- Background: one of six
- Five disposition questions (hidden faction assignment)
- Name entry

On signature commit: hot-wax sound. Name inscribed at bottom of the 900 list.

### Act III — The Taking

20-30 second illustrated scene based on background. 2-3 variations per background (18 total). The Takers (from Act 0) arrive to take the falling Witness into service. Signet pressed into life. Smash to black.

*The Takers retrieve the dead Witness's signet in Act 0 and take the falling Witness into service in Act III. Same class, bookending the ritual. They carry you out; they carry the next one in.*

### Act IV — The Desk

Silence held. Cabin fades up, top-down. Witness wakes at the desk. Signet on finger (the same signet removed in Act 0). Sevasti's letter open beside the Long Record — originally written to the dead Witness, updated for the new one. Player takes control.

## Six Backgrounds

Each represents a moment the Order found you falling. Each has three Taking variations. Each unlocks a late-game quest about what really happened.

### 1. Former Soldier
Dying on a field. Saved via surgery/prosthetics for service.
- **The Sternum.** Clockwork-iron sternum, brass cage around the heart.
- **The Legs.** Prosthetic legs, Order sigil burned at hip joint.
- **The Only Survivor.** Whole. You don't know what they did to you.
*Gear:* Reinforced grey campaign coat, prosthetic component, sidearm, short blade.

### 2. Disgraced Theologian
Condemned by someone else, saved in exchange for service.
- **The Procession.** Witness stops your march to execution. "The Order has a use for heresy."
- **The Ash.** Your seminary burned. The Order testifies for you.
- **The Correct Translation.** A Witness visits your cell. "Your translation was accurate. That is why you must leave the world that reads it."
*Gear:* Scholar-grey robes, annotated breviary, ink vial and quill, short blade.

### 3. Physician of the Verge
Bound by what you saw.
- **The Touched Child.** You hid one. The Order came anyway. "Forget, and serve."
- **The Plague Ward.** Sixteen-hour shifts on a dying moon. A Witness offered coin and a question: *"Do you want to keep doing this, or do you want to matter?"*
- **The Refused Murder.** You refused to kill a patient the Patriarchy wanted dead. The patient died anyway. The Order protected you.
*Gear:* Grey surgical apron, bone saw, tincture kit, short blade.

### 4. Lapsed Aristocrat
Your house fell. The Order saved the name.
- **The Rival.** A rival's manipulation ruined your house. Wear the grey; your family keeps the name.
- **The Debts.** Creditors at the gate. A Witness paid them for your signature. You signed because your mother was weeping.
- **The Duel.** You killed a man in a duel he challenged you to. The Order called it *useful clarity*.
*Gear:* Fine grey cut with cheap alterations, family dueling blade, signet ring hidden under Order signet, short blade.

### 5. Born in the Orphan Worlds
Destitution, recruited young.
- **The Bread.** You were eight. Plague took the adults. The Order came through with bread.
- **The Watcher.** Gutter child in a ring-city. A Witness watched you steal for three months before approaching.
- **The Ash.** Your village burned. You suspect the Order knew it would burn.
*Gear:* Patched grey, paired wire-handled knives, carved wooden icon from your lost village, short blade.

### 6. No Record
Memory cleansed. You are a returning Witness.
- **The Redacted Entries.** Record contains six years of redacted prior entries. Your handwriting changed after the cleansing.
- **The Pale Room.** You remember a room of pale light and a woman asking you questions. You remember agreeing.
- **The Unchosen Name.** The name is not one you would have chosen, but you don't know what you would have chosen.
*Gear:* Older faded grey, unfamiliar weapon, sealed letter from a prior Witness (you), short blade.

## Camera — Hybrid (Locked)

- **Top-down three-quarter** is the default. Overworld, towns, ship interior, claimant courts, dialogue hubs, exploration. 4-direction movement (8 allowed for diagonal).
- **Side-view platformer** for boss fights only. 5-12 handcrafted setpieces across the whole game. Camera whip-pan transition (~0.8s) with sound cue (bell, door, quill) and parchment-edge vignette echoing a Long Record page turn.
- The camera shift IS the signal: *this is a moment.*

## Combat — Two Systems (Locked)

**Top-down combat.** All regular encounters, mini-bosses, environmental jumpscares, secret optional bosses. Primary + secondary weapon slots, spells, rolls, dodges, iframes, loot drops.

**Side-view combat.** Bosses only. Full platformer rules. Gravity, commitment, Blasphemous-adjacent feel. Runs on the existing M3a platformer substrate. Shared damage model with top-down (HP, stagger, iframes, status all identical).

## Ship — Pen, Not Sword (Locked)

The *Impartial Regard* is a walkable top-down hub, not a vehicle.
- Interior: Cabin (Long Record desk, save anchor), bridge (travel console), hold, galley.
- No offensive weapons. Chaff-launcher only — a defensive lie that buys time to break contact.
- Ship combat as a system is eliminated from scope.
- The Witness's authority is documentary. The ship is a pen.

## Travel — Scripted Every Trip (Locked)

Every ship journey is an authored scene. No generic loading screens. A letter arrives. A companion confronts you. You dream. You find a prior entry in the Long Record. Nothing happens, and that silence means something.

## Enemy Taxonomy (Draft, 2026-04-21)

Six categories, grounded in v6 canon (no Penultima).

1. **Order enforcement** — non-Witness Order members, Inquisitors, Fourth-faction agents disguised as any of the above. You fight members of your own Order.
2. **Surviving Touched** — adults the Order failed to kill. The game's moral horror made flesh. Some can speak.
3. **Claimant forces** — armies, retainers, knights, guards, cults loyal to the nine. You kill people defending what they believe in.
4. **Concord enforcement** — peacekeepers, border patrols, militias. Fragmenting in the emperor's absence.
5. **Wildlife and environmental hazards** — fauna, traps, collapsing ruins. Not every encounter has moral weight.
6. **Secret bosses** — encounters the Order has hidden from itself. Touched adults impossibly old. Former Witnesses who refused to die. Ioran Kest on his Verge moon. Possibly a Thelenor survivor.

Individual enemy design is a future session. Order enforcement drills first when designed.

# PART THREE — ART DIRECTION CANON

## Character Visual Language (Locked)

## Palette — Strict

- Deep shadow: `#1a1410`
- Parchment bone: `#e8dcc4` and `#c9b896`
- Ecclesiastical gold: `#c9a04b` (signets, sacred, title card only)
- Candle warmth: `#f4c57a` (rim-light, selected-option highlight only)
- Rust red: `#6b2818` (thread accent, minor detail only)
- Muted greys for cloth throughout
- **No blue, no green, no saturation** outside character signature colors.

## UI Language — Separate from Character Language

Three visual languages, partitioned by purpose:

1. **Pixel world** — characters, enemies, environments in-game. Chained Echoes aesthetic.
2. **Long Record UI** — illuminated manuscript. Parchment, wax, gold foil, ink. IM Fell English, Cormorant Garamond, Uncial Antiqua typography. Diegetic book.
3. **Title screen** — pure cinematic gallery. Outside both other languages. Non-diegetic. Cinzel typography.

## Typography — Locked

- **Cinzel** — institutional. Title card, menus, formal Order documents.
- **Uncial Antiqua** — intimate sacred text. Long Record volume names, founding rite display, ritual inscriptions.
- **Cormorant Garamond** — prompt text, UI body copy, Cinzel fallback.
- **IM Fell English** — Long Record entry body text.

## Canonized Design Principles

### The Rust-Red Thread
Every Witness's uniform carries rust-red stitching at the collar and cuffs — hand-sewn into every robe as a 900-year tradition. The rust thread is the Order's private pain, stitched into every uniform. Subtle by design. The player doesn't notice until their second or third playthrough. The only chromatic accent permitted on Witness sprites.

### Iconic But Variant
Every Witness immediately recognizable as Order from across a room. No two Witnesses identical.
- **Invariants:** layered grey silhouette, Order signet (three concentric rings), Record-issue short blade at left hip, rust-red thread.
- **Variants:** age, build, skin tone, hair, background-specific additions, small personal marks.

### Character Distillations

**The Witness:**
- *"You are not a hero. You are a Record."*
- *"You are the Order's memory made flesh."*
- *"They do not fear your blade. They fear your ink."*

**Elara Vesh:**
- *"She has read the letters. She has done the math. She does not fear death. She fears what her death will cost others."*

### Character Signature Colors

Each major character gets at most one small non-palette detail tied to their interiority.
- **Elara — Faded Starlight Blue.** Hair ribbon, wrist thread, letter bookmark. Never imperial display. Memory, not power.
- **The Witness — no signature color.** Lives entirely within the Order's palette. That absence is the character statement.

Future signature colors (Sevasti, claimants, companions) to be decided. Each must be justified by interiority, used only in intimate objects, absent from public contexts.

## AI Partner Roles

1. **Chat partner (Claude):** story, worldbuilding, design, prompts, briefs, handoffs, coordination between tools.
2. **Claude Code in VS Code:** implementation in the repo against existing architecture.
3. **Image generator (GPT Image 2, Midjourney, etc.):** visual assets and concept art.

Files or copy-paste prompts only. Do not pretend to have filesystem access. Reference files by path.

## The Division of Labor

- **GPT's role:** visual generation AND design specification (prose + numeric specs that describe what it designed).
- **Claude Code's role:** implementation in the repo against the existing architecture.
- **Chat partner's role:** story, worldbuilding, design decisions, high-level briefs, coordination.

GPT can write TypeScript that looks plausible but does not know the codebase. It does not know the engine layer, the scene lifecycle, the SceneContext services, the code rules, the save format, or the asset loader. Code GPT produces will fail on first import against the real architecture.

Claude Code knows the codebase. It reads the actual files. It enforces the rules.

Trying to skip the handoff — asking GPT to produce final code, or asking Claude Code to design UI without a spec — produces worse output from both tools.

## Standard Handoff Format

When GPT produces any significant visual deliverable, it should produce TWO outputs in the same session (or across two turns):

1. **Visual asset.**
2. **Design specification** — prose + numeric document describing the visual precisely enough for a developer to implement.

The spec covers:
- Exact numeric values (typography, spacing, timing, color hex codes, resolutions)
- Placement in screen coordinates or percentages
- State behavior and transitions
- Asset file naming and folder paths
- Audio hooks or animation cues
- Anything a developer would otherwise have to guess

The spec is prose, not code. Claude Code translates prose to code.

## Handoff Sequence

1. Chat partner writes a visual brief for GPT.
2. GPT produces the visual asset and the design spec.
3. Visual saved to `docs/production/art-reference/` or `src/assets/`.
4. Spec saved to `docs/production/ui-specs/` or `docs/production/scene-specs/`.
5. Chat partner writes a Claude Code prompt pointing at both.
6. Claude Code implements against the codebase.

## When to Skip the Spec

For reference-only assets that will never be directly implemented (concept art, character identity briefs, mood boards), a spec is not required.

For anything that will eventually become code — title screens, scene compositions, UI elements, sprite pipelines — the spec is required.
