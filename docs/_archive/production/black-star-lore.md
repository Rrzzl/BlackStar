## Opening Sequence — Locked Structure

The pre-play ritual is one continuous experience, not three separate menus. Same book, same candle, same quill from first frame until the game begins. No voiceover in the opening. The Order does not explain itself to new Witnesses.

### Act I — The Bell
- Game launches to an empty page of the Long Record. Silence.
- One low bell strike.
- The Order's founding rite writes itself onto the page in cramped ecclesiastical script, with translation in smaller italics below.
- The rite reads as holy on first pass. On reflection, it is cruel.
- Page turns.

### Act II — The Binding
- Intake form (Witness Creation).
- Full-body illustrated character, not a portrait close-up. Blasphemous visual language.
- Silent except for quill on parchment and a single candle flickering.
- Player completes the form. The page of nine hundred prior Witnesses scrolls past, their names in the same cramped script.
- The player's chosen name is inscribed at the bottom, in the hand they chose.

### Act III — The Taking
- A short illustrated scene based on the player's chosen background. 20-30 seconds.
- 2-3 variations per background, selected at random. No two playthroughs share an induction.
- Determines starting Order-issued gear and outfit.
- Ends with the signet pressed into your life. Smash to black.

### Act IV — The Desk
- You wake at the desk in the Cabin. The signet is on your finger.
- Sevasti's letter waits. The game begins.

## The Order — Reframed

The Order is not cartoonishly evil. They are arithmeticians. Their founding premise: the galaxy collapses without the Vesh line; preserving the line is holy; what preserves the line is permissible. For nine hundred years they have done the math. The math has always required more.

### Method of Recruitment

The Order does not orchestrate tragedy. They *wait* for it and arrive at the right moment. They are the vultures of circumstance.

- They watch battlefields, plague wards, condemned cells, ruined houses.
- They know before anyone else who is about to fall.
- When you fall, they are there — with surgery, with coin, with a signet, with mercy, whichever is needed.
- You are not a victim of the Order. You are a victim of the world whom the Order happened to save. You will owe them for it. You will not be allowed to forget.

### Internal Composition

Within the Order's ranks, moral axes run in every direction:

- **The Reformers** — genuine believers who have spent decades trying to change the Order from within. Old, tired, mostly unsuccessful. Some disappeared quietly.
- **The Butchers** — those who enjoy the cruelty. The robes are cover.
- **The Merchants** — selling signet authority, indulging corruption for profit.
- **The Liturgists** — true believers in the math. The Order's spine. Neither cruel nor kind. Exhausted.
- **The Hollow** — Witnesses who have stopped believing anything but cannot leave.

Mother Sevasti is a Liturgist. She has poisoned, forged, lied, killed. She remembers every one. She is not cruel, and she will do it again tomorrow.

### Faction Overlay (from v5 Bible)

Above the temperamental grouping, the four named factions still apply:
- **Orthodox** — the line holds, no exceptions.
- **Prescient** — the line holds through intervention. Sevasti's faction.
- **Dissolved** — the line should be allowed to end cleanly.
- **The Fourth** (hidden) — loyal to Korien Vael's original logic.

Individual Witnesses hold both a temperament (Liturgist, Butcher, etc.) and a faction. A Butcher Orthodox and a Butcher Dissolved are different kinds of dangerous.

## The Six Backgrounds

Each background represents a moment in a person's life when the Order arrived and offered a way out. The person took it. They did not yet know the cost.

### Former Soldier

Dying on a field the Order did not choose. Saved in exchange for service.

**Taking variations:**
1. **The Sternum.** Gut wound on a frontier campaign. Order takers find you before the crows. You wake with a clockwork-iron sternum and a brass cage around your heart. You can feel it tick when you lie still.
2. **The Legs.** Cavalry charge failed. Your horse fell on you and shattered both legs. The Order pays the field medics to look away. You wake with prosthetic legs, Order sigil burned into the hip joint.
3. **The Only Survivor.** You lived when two hundred others did not. You cannot explain why. The Order takes men who cannot explain why they lived. You wake whole. You do not know what they did to you, only that they did.

**Order-issued gear:** Reinforced grey campaign coat. Surgical-prosthetic limb component (tracked stat). Standard Witness sidearm. Record-issue short blade.

### Disgraced Theologian

Condemned by someone else, saved by the Order in exchange for service.

**Taking variations:**
1. **The Procession.** Your heresy was real — you wrote it. The Patriarch's court condemned you. On the march to execution, a Witness stops the procession with a signet. *"The Order has a use for heresy."*
2. **The Ash.** Your seminary burned. You alone survived. The Patriarch suspects arson; you did not set it. The Order offers testimony at your trial in exchange for your hand.
3. **The Correct Translation.** You translated a forbidden text accurately and told someone. Three days from the pyre, a Witness visits your cell at night. *"Your translation was accurate. That is why you must leave the world that reads it."*

**Order-issued gear:** Scholar-grey robes over grey. Annotated breviary (readable in-game). Ink vial and quill as a light weapon. Record-issue short blade.

### Physician of the Verge

Bound to the Order by what you saw, not what you did.

**Taking variations:**
1. **The Touched Child.** A noble family brought a sick child to your frontier station. The child was Touched. You hid them. The Order arrived regardless. They offered: forget, and serve.
2. **The Plague Ward.** Sixteen-hour shifts on a dying moon. A Witness came with a signet, a bag of coin, and a question. *"Do you want to keep doing this, or do you want to matter?"* You were tired.
3. **The Refused Murder.** You refused to kill a patient the Patriarchy wanted dead. The patient died anyway. The Order protected you. You did not ask them to.

**Order-issued gear:** Grey surgical apron over grey. Bone saw (weapon). Tincture kit. Record-issue short blade.

### Lapsed Aristocrat

Your house fell. The Order saved your name in exchange for service.

**Taking variations:**
1. **The Rival.** A rival's manipulation ruined your house. The Order did not ruin you, but they were watching. On eviction day, a Witness arrived. Wear the grey; your family keeps the name.
2. **The Debts.** Your father's debts, not yours. Creditors at the gate. A Witness paid them in exchange for your signature. You did not read the document. You signed because your mother was weeping.
3. **The Duel.** You killed a man in a duel he challenged you to. His family called it murder. The Order called it *useful clarity* and offered you a new name for the old one.

**Order-issued gear:** Fine grey cut with cheap alterations. Family dueling blade (personalized per run). Signet ring with your family's original mark hidden under the Order's. Record-issue short blade.

### Born in the Orphan Worlds

Destitution. Recruited young.

**Taking variations:**
1. **The Bread.** You were eight. Plague took the adults. The Order's orphan-takers came through the ruins with bread. You followed.
2. **The Watcher.** You were a gutter child in a ring-city. You stole to eat. A Witness watched you steal for three months before approaching. She said you had "clean hands, despite their color." You did not understand.
3. **The Ash.** Your village burned. You do not remember who burned it. The Order found you in the ash and raised you. You have always suspected they knew it would burn. You have never been able to prove it.

**Order-issued gear:** Patched grey. A pair of wire-handled knives (paired weapon). A carved wooden icon from your lost village. Record-issue short blade.

### No Record

Memory cleansed. You are a returning Witness, not a new one.

**Taking variations:**
1. **The Redacted Entries.** You wake at the desk. The Record contains six years of your prior entries. They are redacted. Even your handwriting changed after the cleansing.
2. **The Pale Room.** You remember a room of pale light and a woman asking you questions you cannot now recall. You remember agreeing to something. You are here because of that agreement.
3. **The Unchosen Name.** The name in the Record is not one you would have chosen for yourself, but you do not know what you would have chosen. The Witness you were had reasons.

**Order-issued gear:** Standard grey, older than the other backgrounds'. A weapon you do not remember acquiring. Record-issue short blade. A sealed letter from a prior Witness (you) addressed to you, still unopened.

### Design Rule

Every background unlocks a late-game quest line where the Witness discovers what the Order — or the world — actually did. Some Witnesses find out and break. Some find out and harden. Some never find out. That is the player's call per playthrough.

## Art Direction

### Character Visual Language — Locked

- **Style:** Sharp pixel art, no anti-aliasing, crunchy edges. Closer to Chained Echoes / Lisa the Painful than Blasphemous or Hollow Knight. Blasphemous is too ambitious for solo production and fights the gothic-melancholy tone we want anyway.
- **Scale:** 32-40 pixels tall per character. Big enough for a belted robe, a visible signet, a weapon at the hip. Small enough to animate without pain.
- **Proportions:** Slightly heroic — not chibi. Head-to-body ratio roughly 1:3.5. No smiling. Faces read as weathered, not cute.
- **Palette:** Long Record palette is the base — deep shadow `#1a1410`, parchment bone `#e8dcc4` `#c9b896`, ecclesiastical gold `#c9a04b`, candle warmth `#f4c57a`, rust `#6b2818`. Mostly locked with rare story-beat exceptions (Elara may carry one non-palette color; a claimant's throne room may push one unusual hue for visual shock). Muted, not monochrome.
- **Faces:** Visible by default. Two-pixel eyes, one-pixel mouths, readable expression. Hooded alternate sprite states exist for ceremony, stealth, and Order rites.
- **Construction:** Full-body standing figures. Strong silhouette. Lit implicitly from a single off-frame candle — rim highlight one side, contact shadow below.

### Camera — Hybrid, Locked

- **Top-down (three-quarter) for:** overworld travel, ship cabin, claimant courts, towns, dialogue hubs, Elara's library, the Tessarene bindery, Sevasti's chambers.
- **Side-view platformer for:** dungeons, boss encounters, any scene that turns violent.
- **Transition between modes** is a designed beat — fade with a sound (door opening, bell, quill setting down), Witness walks into side-view frame from behind. The camera shift is itself a tonal signal: *this is going to hurt now*.
- **Existing platformer substrate** (M3a — TileMap, Physics2D, Collision, Controller) lives in side-view mode and retains full value.
- **New top-down controller** must be built — 4-direction movement, no gravity, tile-edge collision. SceneContext needs a mode flag.

### Sprite Burden Per Character

- **Characters in both modes** (Witness, Elara if extracted, any companion): top-down front/back/left/right walk cycles + side-view left/right walk, idle, attack, hit, roll. Roughly 10-12 sprite states each.
- **Characters in top-down only** (Sevasti, shopkeepers, most NPCs): 4 walk cycles + idle. Roughly 5 states.
- **Characters in side-view only** (enemies, bosses): idle, walk, attack variants, hit, death. Roughly 6-8 states.

### UI Language (from v5)
- Long Record aesthetic locked: IM Fell English (entries), Cormorant Garamond (prompt text), Uncial Antiqua (sacred titles).
- Parchment, wax, gold foil, ink. Illustrated UI layer, not pixel.
- The game's rendered pixel/sprite layer is separate from the UI layer. Two visual languages, clearly partitioned. The Long Record is the game's diegetic book; the world is pixel.

### Asset Pipeline

1. **Concept generation** — AI image generator (GPT-Image / Midjourney / Gemini) for first-pass sprite references. Locked style prompt (next session's work).
2. **Sprite production** — Aseprite for hand-pixeling using AI references as guide. AI rarely produces game-ready sprite sheets; it produces good single-pose references that a human cleans up and animates.
3. **Animation** — frame-by-frame in Aseprite. 4-6 frames per walk cycle. 2-4 frames per attack. Minimum viable movement.
4. **Integration** — PNG sprite sheets into `src/assets/sprites/`. Naming convention: `{character}_{mode}_{state}_{frame}.png`. Claude Code reads paths, renders via engine.
5. **Blender** — not required. Revisit only if specific characters need consistent multi-angle animation beyond what hand-pixeling can sustain.

## Combat Model — Locked

Two combat systems, distinguished by camera and by encounter type.

### Top-Down Combat — Default

Used for all non-boss encounters: regular enemies, mini-bosses, environmental jumpscares during exploration, secret optional bosses hidden in the world.

- 4-direction movement (8 permitted for diagonal feel)
- Primary and secondary weapon slots, equipable from inventory
- Spells as a third capability, slot-limited
- Roll / dodge with iframes
- Loot drops from enemies
- Runs on the top-down controller

### Side-View Boss Combat — Reserved

Used only for handcrafted boss encounters, roughly 5-12 in the whole game. Each boss is a setpiece.

- Camera shift is the signal: *this is a moment*
- Full platformer rules. Gravity, jumping, commitment
- Weight and spacing matter. Blasphemous-adjacent feel
- Player is expected to die and learn
- Runs on the existing platformer substrate from M3a
- Shared damage model with top-down: HP, stagger, iframes, status all identical

### Camera Transition

When the player enters a boss arena, the camera whip-pans from top-down to side-view over ~0.8 seconds. Sound cue (bell, door, quill dropped). Parchment-edge vignette sweeps across the screen during the transition, visually echoing the Long Record's page turn. The camera shift itself is diegetic — the Record is "turning the page" to a new kind of scene.

This transition is the game's signature moment. Worth designing deliberately when the first boss is built.

## Ship Design — Locked

### The Impartial Regard as Hub

The ship is a walkable top-down interior, not a vehicle UI. The player enters the ship from a planet, walks through its rooms, interacts with objects and companions, approaches the bridge console to select a destination.

- **Cabin** — the Witness's quarters. Contains the desk and the Long Record. This is the save point and narrative anchor.
- **Main deck / bridge** — the travel console, starfield windows, a pilot's chair (automated; the Witness is not a pilot).
- **Hold** — inventory overflow, cargo, companions may rest here.
- **Galley / common room** — companion interactions, overheard conversations, atmosphere.

### Pen, Not Sword

The *Impartial Regard* is unarmed. No offensive weapons. The only defensive tool is a chaff-launcher — a sensor-scrambling device that buys time to break contact. It is not a weapon. It is a defensive lie.

The Witness's power is documentary, not violent. The Long Record is their only real authority. The ship's weaponlessness is thematic: the Witness cannot shoot their way out of anything. Space encounters, when they happen, are survived by evasion, deception, or dialogue.

This eliminates ship combat as a system. Space encounters, when they occur, are scripted narrative puzzles — not playable combat. The player's tools are chaff, conversation, and flight.

### Scripted Ship Travel

Every trip between locations is an authored scene. No generic loading screens.

- Every planetary jump triggers a scene beat. Some are short (a few sentences of flavor, a Long Record entry written, a companion's passing remark). Some are full sequences (a letter from Sevasti, a dream, a confrontation with a companion, a discovered entry from a previous Witness, a Fourth-faction ambush handled narratively).
- Scenes vary in camera framing by narrative need: top-down bird's-eye of the ship through starlight, POV inside the cabin as the Witness writes, a Long Record page composition taking over the screen. Camera serves the moment.
- If nothing has changed since the last trip between the same two points, a spare flavor-text transition is permitted. Silence is a scene too.
- Trip scenes are the primary vehicle for character development, internal monologue, and world-state reveals outside of planet visits.

### What This Eliminates from Scope

- Ship combat (weapons, shields, enemy ship AI, dogfight physics) — not built
- Piloting UI, navigation minigame — not built
- Space sector simulation (trade routes, economy, territorial control) — already archived, stays archived
- Resource management in transit (fuel, food, crew morale) — not built

### What This Commits to Build

- A top-down ship interior scene (or set of scenes, one per room)
- A travel scene system — scripted sequences keyed to origin, destination, and story-state flags
- The chaff-launcher as a one-interaction tool for specific scripted encounters
- A travel console UI that reads as diegetic (a table with a star map, not a menu)

### The Witness as Singular Position

The Witness is not merely a rank within the Order — it is the Order's apex position, and only one Witness is alive at a time. The full internal hierarchy of the Order is not yet designed (lower classes with their own jobs, names, and authority structures remain to be fleshed out), but the structural fact of Witness-as-singular is locked:

- The Order at all times has exactly one Witness.
- When a Witness dies, the Order has no Witness until the replacement is made via the Body-Bell-Binding ritual.
- This means the player's Witness is unique in the galaxy while alive. Not one Witness among many — *the* Witness.
- Sevasti's letters, claimant receptions, and Order internal operations all assume a single Witness at a time.
- Ending ζ's exposure-of-the-forgery carries additional weight under this rule: while the Order purges and replaces the disgraced Witness, the Order is *without* a Witness, which has no historical precedent and no institutional procedure for.

The full Order hierarchy — lower ranks, enforcer classes (who retrieve bodies, strip signets, perform Takings), administrative tiers, the seniority system that feeds Witnesses upward from somewhere — is a dedicated future session.

## Canonized Design Principles

Character-level design doctrine that emerged in the 2026-04-21 art session. These are locked and govern all future character work.

### The Rust-Red Thread

Every Witness's uniform carries rust-red stitching at the collar and cuffs — hand-sewn into every robe as a 900-year tradition no one explains aloud. The palette already contains rust (`#6b2818`) as the permitted minor accent. Now it has doctrine: the rust thread is **the Order's private pain, stitched into every uniform**. Subtle by design. The player doesn't notice until their second or third playthrough. When they notice, it reframes every Witness they've ever seen.

The rust thread is the only chromatic accent permitted on Witness sprites. It is not decorative — it is the Order sewing its own cost into its people.

### Iconic But Variant

Every Witness must be *immediately* recognizable as Order from across a room. No two Witnesses may be identical. The design solution:

**Invariants (shared across every Witness, 900 years):**
- Layered grey robe silhouette — identical shape on every Witness regardless of body
- The Order signet at chest (three concentric rings, muted ecclesiastical gold)
- Record-issue short blade at left hip (plain, functional, identical across all Witnesses)
- Rust-red thread at collar and cuffs

**Variants (permitted individual difference):**
- Age bracket (Young / Tempered / Elder)
- Build (slender / average / heavy)
- Skin tone (full range)
- Hair (styles, lengths, colors — always tied back, pulled, or cropped; never loose or ornamental)
- Background-specific additions layered over the base grey (campaign coat, scholar robes, surgical apron, etc. — overlays, not replacements)
- Small personal marks: prayer knot, worn icon on cord, scars, worn book of hours

The test: three Witnesses standing together must read as *all clearly Order, all clearly different people.*

### Character Distillations

Locked as in-world or authorial truth about major characters. These are the one-line summaries the game's art direction and narrative must both serve.

**The Witness:**
- *"You are not a hero. You are a Record."*
- *"You are the Order's memory made flesh."*
- *"They do not fear your blade. They fear your ink."*

**Elara Vesh:**
- *"She has read the letters. She has done the math. She does not fear death. She fears what her death will cost others."*

### Character Signature Colors

Each major character is permitted at most one small non-palette detail that is *theirs alone* — a visual signature tied to their interiority, not their office.

- **Elara Vesh — Faded Starlight Blue.** Used only in intimate, personal objects: her hair ribbon, wrist thread, letter bookmark, the night sky she studies. Never in imperial display. The color of memory, not power.
- **The Witness — No signature color.** The Witness lives entirely within the Order's palette. The rust-red thread IS the accent, and it is not personal — it is the Order's mark on them. That the Witness has no color of their own is itself the character statement.

Future signature colors (Sevasti, claimants, companions) to be decided as characters are designed. Each must be justified by interiority, used only in intimate objects, and absent from public or institutional contexts.
